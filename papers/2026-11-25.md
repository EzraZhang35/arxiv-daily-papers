### 1. Rethinking Reasoning Effort Training in Large Language Models: On-Policy Distillation or Reinforcement Learning?

- **arXiv ID**: [2611.32131](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32131v1)
- **Authors**: Tianliang Lv, Hao Li, Xinchao Xu, Zhengkun Zhang, Ju Huo
- **Categories**: cs.LG
- **Published**: 2026-11-24
- **Relevance**: title:on-policy distillation, title:distillation, title:large language model, abstract:on-policy distillation, abstract:distillation, abstract:agent
- **Abstract**: Large language models have shown strong capabilities on complex tasks, yet how to control their reasoning effort remains a significant challenge for practical deployment. This paper provides a systematic investigation of reasoning-effort training, contrasting reinforcement learning (RL) with on-policy distillation (OPD). We find that compressing reasoning and separating effort tiers are governed by different signals. Length-penalized RL reliably compresses a single policy, reducing thinking on AIME25 by 70\% without accuracy loss, but collapses the tiers when they are trained jointly. Multi-teacher OPD separates them: across 33 benchmarks, low and medium use 58\% and 43\% fewer thinking tokens than high, while high improves on the reference policy by 3.1 points.

### 2. Branch and Consolidate: A Unified Recipe for Multi-Teacher On-Policy Distillation

- **arXiv ID**: [2611.32515](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32515v1)
- **Authors**: Zihan Liang, Zikun Qu, Ben Chen, Fan Yang, Wenwu Ou, Min Zhang, Zhongxiang Dai, Xinchao Wang
- **Categories**: cs.LG, cs.AI
- **Published**: 2026-11-24
- **Relevance**: title:on-policy distillation, title:distillation, abstract:on-policy distillation, abstract:distillation, title:on-policy
- **Abstract**: Specialists post-trained from a shared checkpoint acquire complementary capabilities, and multi-teacher on-policy distillation (MOPD) can consolidate them into a single model. However, we find that existing consolidation methods, from weight merging to MOPD, fall short when specialists diverge substantially from the shared checkpoint, whereas prior studies mostly examine specialists that remain close to it. We present a unified recipe for multimodal MOPD that consolidates such specialists back into the single student they branched from. The recipe specifies initialization, data selection, balanced supervision across teachers, and preservation of existing capabilities for consolidation, together with a targeted refinement stage for capabilities that still lag behind their specialists. In an industrial setting with six specialists, the student closely approaches each specialist in its own domain and surpasses the perception specialist.

### 3. Describe, Don't Ask: Caption-Driven On-Policy Distillation for Fine-Grained Visual Perception

- **arXiv ID**: [2611.32463](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32463v1)
- **Authors**: Haozhan Shen, Liangbo He, Jun Lan, Huijia Zhu, Weiqiang Wang, Tiancheng Zhao, Jianwei Yin
- **Categories**: cs.CV, cs.AI
- **Published**: 2026-11-24
- **Relevance**: title:on-policy distillation, title:distillation, abstract:on-policy distillation, abstract:distillation, title:on-policy
- **Abstract**: Vision-language models (VLMs) often recognize fine-grained details more accurately in a relevant crop than in the full image. Multimodal on-policy distillation (OPD) methods exploit this by transferring the advantage of a crop-view teacher to a full-image student. However, their supervision relies on region-specific questions curated through an elaborate synthesis pipeline, which brings three weaknesses: (1) every region needs a question--answer pair written exclusively for it, locking training into costly QA curation that is hard to scale to unlabeled images; (2) all rollouts of a region answer the same question, so the teacher supervises only the details it asks about, leaving other visual elements in the region unsupervised; and (3) every question presupposes that its target exists, so the student becomes prone to answering even when the target is absent. In this paper, we study how to turn unlabeled images into broad visual supervision through multi-aspect self-description and privileged-view distillation, and propose CaptionOPD, which replaces tailored questions with a fixed set of image-agnostic caption prompts, ranging from general descriptions to focused captions of parts, colors, counts, text, and layout. The rollouts of a region receive different prompts, so the teacher supervises various aspects of the region.

- **Published**: 2026-11-24
- **Relevance**: title:on-policy distillation, title:distillation, abstract:on-policy distillation, abstract:distillation, title:on-policy
- **Abstract**: Flow-based diffusion models have become a mainstream paradigm for text-to-image generation, where task-specific post-training produces specialist models with complementary strengths. ### 5. Verifiable On-Policy Distillation

- **arXiv ID**: [2611.30325](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.30325v1)
- **Authors**: Juntong Shi, Stefano Ermon, Jure Leskovec, Minkai Xu
- **Categories**: cs.LG, cs.CL
- **Published**: 2026-11-23
- **Relevance**: title:on-policy distillation, title:distillation, abstract:on-policy distillation, abstract:distillation, title:on-policy
- **Abstract**: Reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR) has become a powerful approach for improving language-model reasoning, but its supervision is inherently sparse: a sequence-level verifier reward is propagated only through sampled trajectories and provides no direct signal for alternative actions. On-policy distillation (OPD) offers a complementary form of supervision by providing dense teacher feedback over the full next-token distribution, but this signal is not grounded in verified task outcomes. Simply co-optimizing the two objectives does not couple these signals: verifier feedback remains sparse and does not inform the dense teacher supervision. We introduce Verifiable On-Policy Distillation (VOPD), which transfers sparse verifier feedback into dense, verifier-grounded supervision. Our key observation is that the implicit-reward view of OPD places teacher supervision and verifier feedback in a common reward space while retaining a reward signal available for every next-token action.

- **Abstract**: The growing autonomy of AI agents in using external tools and making decisions has created an urgent need for comprehensive safety benchmarks. ### 13. Contrastive On-Policy Self-Distillation for LLM Unlearning

- **arXiv ID**: [2611.31932](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31932v1)
- **Authors**: Fangzhou Lin, Shuo Xing, Xin Li, Wenbin He, Zhengzhong Tu, Liu Ren
- **Categories**: cs.AI
- **Published**: 2026-11-24
- **Relevance**: title:distillation, title:LLM, abstract:distillation, title:on-policy
- **Abstract**: Large language model unlearning aims to remove the influence of designated training data while preserving unrelated model capabilities. Existing likelihood-based methods commonly optimize against fixed forget sequences, providing only negative supervision on what the model should not generate and overlooking the states visited by the evolving unlearned policy. We introduce Contrastive On-Policy Self-Distillation (CPSD), a new unlearning framework that constructs an explicit knowledge-reduced target distribution and distills it along the model's own trajectories. ### 15. fork() for Agents: Agent Kernel for Non-blocking LLM Orchestration

- **arXiv ID**: [2611.31727](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31727v1)
- **Authors**: Ankit Chadha, Pratyay Banerjee
- **Categories**: cs.CL, cs.DC
- **Published**: 2026-11-24
- **Relevance**: title:agent, title:LLM, abstract:agent
- **Abstract**: LLM-based agents typically execute tool calls synchronously and discard completed work upon failure. Subgraph-selective recovery, re-executing only a failed node's transitive descendants, is a well-established pattern in distributed systems (Spark, Erlang/OTP, Dryad), and recent agent runtimes (Sherlock, AgentGit, Atomix) preserve completed work through verifiers, version control, or transactional tool boundaries. We present \textbf{Agent-Kernel}, a non-blocking runtime that ports this pattern to the LLM \emph{content plane}: its primitive, \textbf{Sub-Graph Recovery (SGR)}, fires on a raw exception with no trained verifier or planner, resets a failed node's descendants while preserving completed siblings, and composes each parent's text into its children's prompts (context inheritance), all on a durable substrate with a formal state-preservation guarantee. Across 14{,}434 runs on synthetic and standard benchmarks (tau-bench, Gaia2, AutomationBench, InterruptBench), SGR reaches full recovery within budget=3 where whole-trajectory replay exhausts (100\% vs.\ 55\%), and is roughly 10$\times$ faster than an adapted Sherlock rollback that also recovers. It matches ReAct-serial accuracy within noise on linear workloads while adding recovery and parallelism (up to 12.7$\times$ speedup) where structure permits.

### 16. Attributing Security Outcomes in LLM-Agent Evaluations: The Role of Content-Free State

- **arXiv ID**: [2611.30259](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.30259v1)
- **Authors**: Abdelmonaim Bounite, Zangir Iklassov
- **Categories**: cs.CR, cs.AI, cs.LG
- **Published**: 2026-11-23
- **Relevance**: title:agent, title:LLM, abstract:agent
- **Abstract**: Security benchmarks for LLM agents record whether each attack succeeded and compare agents, attacks, and defenses by attack success rate (ASR). Such comparisons are valid only if the agent, the attack, and the defense cause each verdict. We show that verdicts also depend on content-free state: details of a run that carry no information about the system under test, such as the Python hash seed, the order of tools or evidence, and the scoring of failed runs. Our framework, TARE (Testing Attribution of Reported Effects), attributes a reported effect to the agent, attack, or defense only if it exceeds the measured influence of content-free state.

### 18. Bathys: Advancing Deep Search Agents

- **arXiv ID**: [2611.32660](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32660v1)
- **Authors**: Zixuan Chen, Shengyou Yuan, Nang Yuan, Ming Ma, Zelin Xiao, Lin Zhou, Siyuan Luo, Jiaqi Su
  *(and 5 more)*
- **Categories**: cs.CL, cs.AI
- **Published**: 2026-11-24
- **Relevance**: title:agent, abstract:tool use, abstract:agent
- **Abstract**: Deep search is a critical capability for agents tackling complex, open-ended tasks, where solving a problem requires iterative information gathering, query reformulation, and evidence-based verification rather than a single retrieval step. To address these requirements, we present Bathys 9B and 27B deep search agents built around a training framework that spans verifiable question synthesis, post-training, and online reinforcement learning (RL) under context compression.
