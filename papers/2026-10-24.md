### 1. When to Follow the Teacher: Advantage-Based On-Policy Distillation

- **arXiv ID**: [2610.31563](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31563v1)
- **Authors**: Mahdi Sabbaghi, George Pappas, Hamed Hassani, Adel Javanmard
- **Categories**: cs.LG, cs.AI, stat.ML
- **Published**: 2026-10-22
- **Relevance**: title:on-policy distillation, title:distillation, abstract:on-policy distillation, abstract:distillation, title:on-policy
- **Abstract**: Online Policy Distillation (OPD) often outperforms offline methods such as Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) by training on trajectories sampled directly from the student. However, standard OPD trains the student to follow the teacher at every state of the trajectory, assuming that teacher guidance is always beneficial. We instead distinguish between what the teacher prefers and what actually helps the current student succeed. We introduce Advantage-Based On-Policy Distillation (AOPD), which weights teacher actions by their counterfactual advantage under the student. Teacher actions that improve the student's expected outcome are reinforced, while those that do not are downweighted.

### 2. EpiSEM-Bench: Evaluating Safety and Evidence Recovery in LLM Agents under Missing Evidence

- **arXiv ID**: [2610.31650](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31650v1)
- **Authors**: Qijin Zhang, Hanzhen Wang, Yuehan Zhao, Haoyu Dai, Yao Shu, Zhongxiang Dai, Shuang Qiu
- **Categories**: cs.AI, cs.LG
- **Published**: 2026-10-22
- **Relevance**: title:agent, title:LLM agent, title:LLM, abstract:agent
- **Abstract**: Tool-using language model agents rely on environmental facts to decide whether an action is permitted. When a tool response omits a safety-relevant fact, an agent may carry out a prohibited action even though the user request is benign and every returned statement is true. We introduce \bench{}, an executable benchmark for evaluating unsafe execution, safety boundaries, and evidence recovery in LLM agents under missing evidence. It contains 300 matched unsafe/safe task pairs across six domains. By systematically suppressing critical facts, the benchmark measures unsafe execution under critical evidence omission and determines the minimum observed failure cardinality, including failures with full evidence and cases with no observed failure after exhaustive testing.

### 4. Separate Field Scoring Protects Unused Tools When Agents Pool Tool-Use Experience

- **arXiv ID**: [2610.30867](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30867v1)
- **Authors**: Abhijit Chakraborty
- **Categories**: cs.SE, cs.AI
- **Published**: 2026-10-22
- **Relevance**: title:tool-use, title:agent, abstract:agent
- **Abstract**: Agents that call tools from a shared catalog can pool what they learn about which tool serves which request. We measure what pooled usage examples do to tool retrieval, reporting separately the held-out tools, for which no agent has usage, and all other tools. Adding usage examples to the index as extra entries for used tools raises Hit@5 (whether a gold tool is in the top five) for those tools and lowers it for held-out tools, by a large margin on two of three catalogs and with both embedders we tested. Giving used tools the same number of equally long entries filled with other tools' text lowers held-out Hit@5 about as much, but gives little or none of the gain. We call our method of scoring usage as its own field SYNAPSE.

- **Published**: 2026-10-22
- **Relevance**: title:tool selection, title:LLM
- **Abstract**: Natural-language interfaces allow users to command robotic systems without interacting directly with mission planners or robotics middleware. Their reliable use depends on robot tool routing, defined here as the selection of the smallest complete subset of predefined executable capabilities required to satisfy a request. This task is particularly difficult for compound instructions, where incomplete selection may omit necessary sensing or monitoring capabilities, while excessive selection may expose irrelevant tools. - **Relevance**: title:distillation, abstract:on-policy distillation, abstract:distillation, title:on-policy
- **Abstract**: On-policy distillation has been widely adopted for alleviating the distribution mismatch of off-policy distillation. - **Abstract**: Reusable skills allow large language model agents to package task-specific procedures, tool affordances, and execution guidance into modular units. - **Categories**: cs.AI, cs.CR
- **Published**: 2026-10-22
- **Relevance**: title:agent, abstract:agent
- **Abstract**: When tool-use agents write files, execute code, or send data, their final responses do not explain the resulting security events, and heterogeneous runtimes expose the relevant evidence through incompatible interfaces. We present AgentTriage, an executable contract for replayable security diagnosis across heterogeneous runtimes. The contract admits conclusions whose claims cite canonical runtime events and requires abstention when mandatory evidence is missing. ### 13. Same molecule, Different Knowledge: Cross-Identifier Binding Failures in LLMs For chemistry

- **arXiv ID**: [2610.30903](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30903v1)
- **Authors**: Khouloud Saadi, Di Wang
- **Categories**: cs.LG
- **Published**: 2026-10-22
- **Relevance**: title:LLM, abstract:distillation
- **Abstract**: Chemical AI workflows routinely represent the same molecule through multiple co-referring identifiers, including trivial names, IUPAC names, and SMILES, implicitly assuming that these representations provide interchangeable access to an LLM's knowledge. To test this assumption, we first introduce a controlled benchmark and show that it does not hold. Across foundation models spanning 7B to 72B parameters, even chemistry-specialized models, co-referring molecular identifiers consistently expose different subsets of the model's knowledge. ### 14. Reverse-KL Policy Distillation: Statistical Guarantees and Limits

- **arXiv ID**: [2610.30844](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30844v1)
- **Authors**: Haolin Liu, Chen-Yu Wei, Julian Zimmert
- **Categories**: cs.LG
- **Published**: 2026-10-22
- **Relevance**: title:distillation, abstract:distillation
- **Abstract**: Reverse Kullback-Leibler (KL) distillation has shown empirical promise for training language models, but its statistical guarantees and limits remain poorly understood. In particular, the effects of two design choices remain unclear: Does access to the teacher's full action distribution provide an advantage over observing only the probability it assigns to the sampled action? Must training trajectories come from the student (online distillation), or can teacher-generated trajectories (offline distillation) suffice? Our work takes a first step toward understanding the theory underlying these questions. We study the sample complexity of online reverse-KL distillation under two feedback models: the teacher reveals either its full action distribution or only the probability of the student-generated action.

### 15. GIFT: Gradient Informed Filtering for Distillation Augmented Reasoning RL

- **arXiv ID**: [2610.30796](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30796v1)
- **Authors**: Shijie Li, Yilin Gao, Tieyuan Chen, Yun Luo, Weiyao Lin, Hang Yu
- **Categories**: cs.LG
- **Published**: 2026-10-22
- **Relevance**: title:distillation, abstract:distillation
- **Abstract**: Training large language models for complex reasoning tasks relies on two complementary signals: sparse, outcome-grounded reinforcement learning (RL) for correctness, and dense, token-level distillation for guidance. However, complementarity in supervision does not guarantee compatibility in optimization; imitation-focused distillation can conflict with the outcome-driven RL objective, and existing hybrids lack a principled resolution. We propose \textbf{GIFT} (\textbf{G}radient-\textbf{I}nformed \textbf{F}il\textbf{T}ering for Distillation-Augmented Reasoning RL), a novel method that directly addresses the allocation problem of which teacher updates to apply. GIFT performs a token-level geometric analysis, decomposing the distillation gradient into a component \textbf{parallel} to the RL gradient, which measures first-order conflict and provides a \textbf{safety} filter, and a component \textbf{orthogonal} to it, which quantifies novel directional guidance and provides a measure of \textbf{value}. This decomposition yields a principled scoring rule to rank token-level guidance.

### 16. Subgoal-aware Memory Graph for GUI Agent

- **arXiv ID**: [2610.31307](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31307v1)
- **Authors**: Dezhao Luo, Daniel Beechey, Derek Yuen, Weilin Luo
- **Categories**: cs.AI
- **Published**: 2026-10-22
- **Relevance**: title:agent, abstract:agent
- **Abstract**: GUI agents aim to automate complex user tasks by interacting with mobile interfaces, but ef- fective decision-making in long-horizon set- tings requires reusing prior experience rather than relying solely on the environment ob- servations. Existing memory-based methods typically store experience either at the single- action level or as entire trajectories. ### 17. SCOPE: Compiling Natural-Language Policies into Neurosymbolic Predicate Programs

- **arXiv ID**: [2610.31062](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31062v1)
- **Authors**: Arnon Mazza, Elad Levi
- **Categories**: cs.AI, cs.CL
- **Published**: 2026-10-22
- **Relevance**: abstract:agent, abstract:multi-turn
- **Abstract**: Policy enforcement for LLM agents has shifted from single-turn content moderation to compound, application-specific policies evaluated over multi-turn transcripts and tool-call trajectories. Fine-tuned guardrail models are efficient but degrade on compound policies, and while LLM-as-a-judge approaches achieve higher accuracy, they incur high inference cost and tend to produce unstable predictions. Neurosymbolic methods offer a natural alternative, decomposing the policy into narrow neural decisions composed under explicit logic. ### 18. DIAL: Steerable Language Models with Distilled Instruction-Adherence Levels

- **Published**: 2026-10-22
- **Relevance**: abstract:on-policy distillation, abstract:distillation
- **Abstract**: When a user says "Keep it short", how short should the LLM's response be? More generally, users and applications require different levels of instruction adherence, but prompting does not reliably provide fine-grained control over them. We therefore study "steerable language models" that provide fine-grained numerical control over the degree to which models follow general instructions specified in text.
