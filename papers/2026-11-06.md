### 1. EvoOPD: Evolving Privileged Information Strategies for Black-Box On-Policy Distillation

- **arXiv ID**: [2611.06542](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06542v1)
- **Authors**: Ximo Zhu, Ruiqi Liu, Yi Han, Zhengan Wu, Gengsheng Li, ZiAn Wang, Zhu Yuantian, Jin Jiang
  *(and 1 more)*
- **Categories**: cs.LG
- **Published**: 2026-11-04
- **Relevance**: title:on-policy distillation, title:distillation, abstract:on-policy distillation, abstract:distillation, title:on-policy
- **Abstract**: Proprietary language models exhibit strong reasoning and coding capabilities, but their output distributions are inaccessible, making fine-grained distillation methods that rely on teacher logits difficult to apply directly to black-box models. We observe that rich textual feedback from a black-box teacher can be treated as privileged information (PI) and converted, through a PI-conditioned privileged teacher, into dense supervision signals on the student's own trajectories. However, what PI should be acquired from the teacher to most effectively support student learning remains unclear. PI strategies that differ in where they focus, what information they request, and how much detail they provide can have substantially different effects on the current student, and their relative effectiveness can further change as training progresses. Based on this observation, we propose Evolving Privileged Information Strategies for Black-Box On-Policy Distillation (EvoOPD), which treats PI acquisition itself as an optimization problem and maintains a PI Strategy Bank that evolves with the student state.

### 2. Ruby-OPD: Enhancing Pronunciation Controllability and Accuracy for Flow-Matching Text-to-Speech via On-Policy Distillation

- **arXiv ID**: [2611.06403](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06403v1)
- **Authors**: Rui Hu, Xiaolong Lin, Zhenpeng Zhan
- **Categories**: cs.CL
- **Published**: 2026-11-04
- **Relevance**: title:on-policy distillation, title:distillation, abstract:on-policy distillation, abstract:distillation, title:on-policy
- **Abstract**: Achieving both high pronunciation accuracy and fine-grained controllability remains a central challenge in Japanese text-to-speech (TTS), as Japanese is an unsegmented language mixing logographic Kanji and phonographic Kana with extensive polyphony. Traditional cascaded systems convert text into phoneme sequences via a front-end, ensuring pronunciation control but discarding lexical semantics and prosodic cues in surface orthography. Conversely, recent zero-shot TTS models directly consume raw orthographic text to improve naturalness, yet suffer from polyphone errors and lack explicit pronunciation control. To bridge this gap, we propose a two-stage framework for flow-matching TTS. First, we investigate Ruby text representations with orthography perturbation to jointly preserve surface forms and target pronunciations for reliable control.

### 3. Learning When Human Decisions Matter: Executor-Relative Consultation Value for LLM Agents

- **arXiv ID**: [2611.05814](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05814v1)
- **Authors**: Shuli Jiang, Khushbu Pahwa, Yuting Zhang, Yi Zhang, Wei Xia, Stefano Soatto
- **Categories**: cs.CL, cs.AI
- **Published**: 2026-11-04
- **Relevance**: title:agent, title:LLM agent, title:LLM, abstract:distillation, abstract:agent
- **Abstract**: Large language models increasingly automate complex tasks, but human decision capacity does not scale with their capabilities. In many applications, humans remain essential for providing private context, authority, or judgments unavailable to the agent. We formulate human decision capacity allocation: given multiple consultation opportunities and a limited budget, at which decisions should an LLM agent consult a human? The central challenge is estimating consultation value, which is executor-dependent because the same human input may benefit different downstream agents differently. We introduce Consultation Value Distillation (CVD), which estimates consultation values offline using Monte Carlo executor rollouts and distills these values into lightweight predictors for budgeted allocation, requiring no rollout-based value estimation at deployment.

### 4. SecureGARD: Dynamic Ontology-Guided Governance for Secure Tool-Using LLM Agents

- **arXiv ID**: [2611.06682](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06682v1)
- **Authors**: Guoqin Xiong, Shengtao Wen, Xiang Chen, Xinyi Zeng, Zhaoxin Fan, Wei Ye, Xiaofeng Cao, Sheng-Jun Huang
- **Categories**: cs.LG
- **Published**: 2026-11-04
- **Relevance**: title:agent, title:LLM agent, title:LLM, abstract:agent
- **Abstract**: Tool-using large language model (LLM) agents translate model outputs into consequential actions, turning indirect prompt injection into an execution-security problem. Existing defenses constrain tool calls, trajectories, or information flows, but often leave action semantics, state dependencies, and evidence relations implicit, limiting generalization across heterogeneous tools and complicating safe updates. We present \ours{}, a dynamic ontology-guided governance framework. \ours{} grounds heterogeneous calls in a shared semantic representation and combines an executable ontology with episode-level task state to derive deterministic ready, waiting, and forbidden decisions. A waiting decision identifies an unmet but recoverable obligation, enabling bounded replanning without expanding the agent's authorization boundary.

### 5. When Do Summaries Become Instructions? Unsupported Policy in LLM Agents' Self-Written Handoff Summaries

- **arXiv ID**: [2611.06545](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06545v1)
- **Authors**: Wenhui Chu
- **Categories**: cs.CL
- **Published**: 2026-11-04
- **Relevance**: title:agent, title:LLM agent, title:LLM, abstract:agent
- **Abstract**: Long-running LLM agents compact their context, replacing most history with a summary. We ask whether handoff summaries state permissions or requirements the record does not support, whether such sentences change what the next instance (the successor) does, and where they come from. Synthetic tool-use tasks carry a ground-truth permission ledger; same-snapshot replay reruns the successor with those whole sentences replaced by neutral filler. Two AI readers, reading only rule-flagged held-out summaries, confirmed unsupported restrictions in 1.2% and 2.4% of all readable summaries from two open-weight models, and unsupported authorizations in 0.1% and 1.3%. For restrictions, a pre-specified replay test was not significant (p = 0.58); a follow-up, planned after it, found a fragile effect (p = 0.027).

### 6. MA-Mesh: Resilient Multi-Agent Collaboration via Role-based Self-Healing

- **arXiv ID**: [2611.06389](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06389v1)
- **Authors**: JunYong Lee, Woo Kyung Kim, Honguk Woo
- **Categories**: cs.AI
- **Published**: 2026-11-04
- **Relevance**: title:agent, abstract:tool use, abstract:agent
- **Abstract**: Effective collaboration in multi-agent systems requires mechanisms for coordination and adaptability in dynamic and uncertain environments. However, existing multi-agent systems rarely model resilience explicitly, instead relying on ad-hoc output correction that limits systematic, runtime management of robustness. In this work, we propose Multi-Agent Mesh (MA-Mesh), a framework that constructs a role-based self-healing layer on top of a collaboration pattern. Specifically, MA-Mesh generates resilience-aware system specifications and synthesizes them into executable multi-agent policy code. To enhance resilience, we introduce two self-healing mechanisms: Cross-agent reflection, a mechanism that allows agents to evaluate not only their own outcomes but also the performance of their peers, enabling informed behavioral adjustments and collective policy refinement; Role-based failover, a strategy which dynamically reassigns roles and enables agents to substitute for one another when sub-tasks fail or agents underperform, ensuring continuity of task execution.

### 7. Using Experience Twice: Dynamic Process Rubrics for Privileged Token-Level Credit Assignment

- **arXiv ID**: [2611.06543](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06543v1)
- **Authors**: Shumao Wu, Kongcheng Zhang, Jianqiang Huang, Baisheng Lai
- **Categories**: cs.AI
- **Published**: 2026-11-04
- **Relevance**: abstract:distillation, abstract:agent
- **Abstract**: Agent trajectories often contain both successful decisions and failed attempts, yet optimization with sparse trajectory-level rewards struggles to identify which critical decisions should be reinforced or suppressed. We observe that meaningful distinctions among on-policy trajectories can be distilled into natural-language process rubrics and used as process supervision signals. Based on this insight, we propose Online Rubric Self-Distillation (ORSD), which leverages self-derived process rubrics for dense token-level supervision, thereby augmenting outcome-centric reinforcement learning. Specifically, the current policy independently generates multiple rubric bundles at each step according to the same group of trajectories, extracting candidate process rubrics from cross-trajectory behavioral differences. A frozen verifier then evaluates all rubric items against each trajectory in the group, and selects one candidate bundle based on bundle-level alignment between rubric satisfaction and task success.

### 9. EmoReward: Self-Evolving Reward Calibration for Emotional Video Generation RL

- **arXiv ID**: [2611.06518](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06518v1)
- **Authors**: Mingzhe Zheng, Yue Wu, Jing Tan, Chang Zou, Dengyang Jiang, Zeyue Tian, Xuanhua He, Ryan Xu
  *(and 14 more)*
- **Categories**: cs.CV, cs.AI
- **Published**: 2026-11-04
- **Relevance**: abstract:agent
- **Abstract**: Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) has advanced generative model post-training in verifiable domains such as code and mathematics, but evaluating video quality, especially emotional expression, often depends on subjective aesthetics and has no uniquely correct target. Existing approaches typically rely on fixed rewards for visual quality, motion quality, and text alignment that poorly capture emotional intent and can invite reward hacking, while RLHF is difficult to scale and subject to individual aesthetic differences that complicate the calibration of facial, body motion, and chromatic cues across narrative contexts. We introduce EmoReward, a new self-evolving training mechanism that distills evidence reliability from reward traces collected during an actual training run to adaptively guide multi-reward weight allocation in subsequent training by determining which emotional evidence to trust and how much to weight it. EmoReward first plans specific grounding cues from the emotional prompt to guide evaluation, then uses heterogeneous signal primitives to score the generated video during the act stage. Reflective Reward Gating calibrates their contributions through instance-level gates for phase, face confidence, conflict, and subtlety, then renormalizes weights over the active set to produce the sample's fused reward.

### 10. CRESiST: Self-Enhancing Explorative Policy Learning for Simultaneous Speech Translation

- **arXiv ID**: [2611.06501](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06501v1)
- **Authors**: Chenyang Le, Pei Zhang, Yuxiang Chen, Bing Han, Derek F. Wong, Baosong Yang, Yanmin Qian
- **Categories**: cs.CL, cs.SD
- **Published**: 2026-11-04
- **Abstract**: Simultaneous Speech Translation (SimulST) requires balancing translation quality and latency. While Large Language Models (LLMs) excel in offline translation, current LLM-based SimulST systems predominantly rely on prefix-incremental generation. This paradigm suffers from redundant Key-Value cache recomputation and necessitates external Voice Activity Detection to process unbounded streams. To overcome these bottlenecks, we propose an end-to-end interleaved read-write framework built upon an omni-modal LLM. By interleaving speech chunks and text tokens, our architecture achieves full KV cache reuse, natively supporting segmentation-free, infinite-length inference.
