### 1. PROBE: Learning to Audit Policy Compliance in Tool-Using LLM Agents

- **arXiv ID**: [2610.22124](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22124v1)
- **Authors**: Kshitij Mishra, Abhijith Sharma, Nils Lukas, Salem Lahlou
- **Categories**: cs.AI
- **Published**: 2026-10-15
- **Relevance**: title:agent, title:LLM agent, title:LLM, abstract:tool use, abstract:agent, abstract:multi-turn
- **Abstract**: Modern agentic systems extend language models with external tools, enabling them to complete complex tasks through multi-turn interactions. As these agents are deployed in consequential settings, they are often constrained by policies governing pricing, privacy, confirmation, safety, and other requirements. However, existing evaluation methods leave a critical gap: capability benchmarks measure what agents can accomplish, while content-safety red-teaming measures harmful generations, but neither tests whether agents comply with predefined policies during otherwise valid tool use. Such policy violations are difficult to audit because their effects may emerge only after several turns, span multiple tool calls, or surface in downstream systems. We propose PROBE (Policy Rule Observation via Behavioral Exploration), an algorithm for training LLM-based auditors that interact with deployed agents as realistic users and probe them for policy violations through multi-turn, tool-mediated dialogue.

### 2. Learning to Steer LLM Agents and Multi-Agent Systems

- **arXiv ID**: [2610.22316](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22316v1)
- **Authors**: Leyang Shen, Yaxin Gong, Yang Zhang, Chenxiao Fan, Jiakai Wang, Dong Wang, Yang Liu, Fuli Feng
  *(and 2 more)*
- **Categories**: cs.LG, cs.AI, cs.MA
- **Published**: 2026-10-15
- **Relevance**: title:agent, title:LLM agent, title:LLM, abstract:tool use, abstract:agent
- **Abstract**: Large language model (LLM)-based agents and multi-agent systems increasingly tackle complex tasks through long-horizon tool use and inter-agent collaboration. However, these systems are prone to deviating from the overall goal as execution unfolds, which wastes execution resources and leads to undesired outcomes, a failure termed goal drift. In this work, we propose a steering agent that supervises execution on the fly and steers the system back on track when drift occurs. The steering agent observes the execution context, deciding when to intervene with a low-cost fast path and how to intervene with a high-cost slow path. To unlock its steering intelligence, the ability to assess goal drift and generate effective interventions, we jointly train the two paths with reinforcement learning, combining a pairwise ranking objective with group-relative policy optimization (GRPO).

### 3. Rethinking the Role of Tool Graph Memory in Multi-Turn Tool Use

- **arXiv ID**: [2610.21571](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.21571v1)
- **Authors**: Uiji Hwang, Richeng Xuan, Taeuk Kim
- **Categories**: cs.CL, cs.AI
- **Published**: 2026-10-15
- **Relevance**: title:tool use, title:multi-turn, abstract:tool use, abstract:agent
- **Abstract**: Large language model (LLM) agents solve complex tasks by calling external tools over multiple turns. Recent work has proposed tool graph memory, which stores past experience as tool transitions to guide subsequent selection. However, it remains unclear whether this component consistently improves agents' tool use or undermines it. We evaluate tool graph memory on nine models and two benchmarks, finding that it seldom improves significantly over baselines, and that its gains, where present, cost over twice the LLM calls. The memory's guidance is often incorrect, and random or no guidance retains most of the gain, implying that the benefit stems from extra computation rather than the memory itself.

### 4. TRACER: Trajectory-Aware Tool Retrieval for LLM Agents

- **arXiv ID**: [2610.21267](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.21267v1)
- **Authors**: Tanish Kher, Sarvesh DK, Abedelkadir Asi, Sparsha Chennarayapatna Sundaresh, Dezhi Hong
- **Categories**: cs.CL, cs.AI, cs.IR
- **Published**: 2026-10-15
- **Relevance**: title:agent, title:LLM agent, title:LLM, abstract:agent
- **Abstract**: Modern agents select tools from registries, but exposing entire registries incurs high token costs and can impair selection as they grow. We present TRACER, a retrieval front-end that indexes observed tool-call sequences in a trie and ranks continuations using a learned blend of empirical and contextual priors. On a mixed retail-and-telecom $τ^2$ environment, TRACER improves GPT-5.4's tool-selection accuracy by 4.7 points while reducing input tokens per step by 80%, exposing just 5 of 128 tools. In autonomous full-turn execution, it matches GPT-5.4's full-registry task success with 61% fewer input tokens per step. Across six environments and four models, TRACER improves tool-selection accuracy over full-registry selection in 21 of 24 model-environment pairs, with average gains of 6 to 10 points; the three exceptions trail by at most 2.2.

- **Published**: 2026-10-15
- **Relevance**: title:agent, title:agentic, abstract:agent, abstract:multi-turn
- **Abstract**: Large language models (LLMs) are expanding from assisting scientific research and engineering to autonomously developing and improving AI systems, advancing AI for AI (AI4AI) as a pathway toward recursive self-improvement (RSI). - **Published**: 2026-10-15
- **Relevance**: title:agent, title:agentic, abstract:tool use, abstract:agent
- **Abstract**: Deep research agents are increasingly deployed to retrieve precise information from large corpora, yet multi-hop, long-horizon tasks remain challenging. Synthetic data generation has emerged as a scalable solution, with existing pipelines typically expanding seed tasks to increase their apparent depth and breadth. However, this expansion does not reliably translate into necessary reasoning: newly introduced evidence may be only weakly coupled to the answer, creating a mismatch between intended and empirical difficulty. ### 9. Language Is a Security Surface for Agent Guards

- **arXiv ID**: [2610.22244](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22244v1)
- **Authors**: Hasan Demirkiran, Jens Ernstberger
- **Categories**: cs.CR, cs.AI, cs.CL, cs.LG
- **Published**: 2026-10-15
- **Relevance**: title:agent, abstract:agent
- **Abstract**: Step-level guards decide, before each tool call, whether an agent's next action is safe given the user's request and the trajectory so far. Users write those requests in many languages, while guard prompts and the benchmarks on which purpose-built guards are trained and evaluated are English. We ask whether a guard's verdict depends on the language of the request. We build LinguaGuardBench from the 1,220 labelled steps of TS-Bench AgentDojo-Traj: we translate only the user instruction into German, French, Turkish, Swahili and Amharic, and keep every tool call, observation and label byte-identical, so any change in a verdict is attributable to language alone. Six English rewordings and a whitespace placebo serve as controls, and managed guards are run three times to measure their noise.

### 10. Trajectory-Level Speculation with Semantic Verification for Tool-Using Agents

- **arXiv ID**: [2610.21463](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.21463v1)
- **Authors**: Zilin Wang, Yinhong Liu, Chao Chen, Qun Liu, Zhijiang Guo
- **Categories**: cs.CL, cs.AI, cs.LG
- **Published**: 2026-10-15
- **Relevance**: title:agent, abstract:agent
- **Abstract**: Large language model agents solve tasks through long sequences of tool calls, and the prevailing design has a single strong model generate every step, although many steps are routine enough for a much smaller model. Existing ways of sharing this work keep the strong model as the generator: routers and collaborative agents decide which model handles each query or step, and speculative methods check a small model's drafts one step at a time, usually against the strong model's own action. We introduce SpecAgent, a collaboration protocol that turns the strong model from the author of each step into a reviewer of steps drafted by a weak model. The weak model executes read-only tool calls as it drafts, so that every step is grounded in real observations, and holds the first action that would modify the environment. The strong model then verifies the drafted segment in a single prefill pass, commits the longest prefix it judges semantically valid, and repairs the first rejected step.

### 11. MemScope: Evolving Agent Memory Architectures through Localized Code Repair

- **arXiv ID**: [2610.21170](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.21170v1)
- **Authors**: Miao Su, Yucan Guo, Zixuan Li, Long Bai, Zhongni Hou, Xiaolong Jin, Jiafeng Guo
- **Categories**: cs.AI
- **Published**: 2026-10-15
- **Relevance**: title:agent, abstract:agent
- **Abstract**: Agent memory architectures define how LLM agents store and reuse experience across tasks, but they are typically fixed after deployment, limiting their adaptability across task types. Recent self-evolving approaches instead adapt these memory architectures using execution feedback, typically represented as evaluation scores or compressed summaries. However, such coarse-grained feedback is insufficient for precise failure localization in current agent systems, where long-horizon task execution involves multiple interacting components. Without a precise failure location, existing approaches lack a clear code-level target for modification, leaving the update scope weakly constrained and often leading to large-scale rewrites of an otherwise capable memory architecture. We propose MemScope, a framework for evidence-grounded, localized evolution of an existing memory architecture.

### 12. Planning Commitment of LLM under Partial Observability

  *(and 1 more)*
- **Categories**: cs.AI, cs.CL
- **Published**: 2026-10-15
- **Relevance**: title:LLM, abstract:agent
- **Abstract**: LLM-based planning agents commit to plans of different lengths before interacting with a partially observable environment. Some aggressively commit to a long plan based on assumptions about unobserved states, while others conservatively plan only over what they have observed. Both behaviors emerge implicitly in pre-trained models, whether the LLM outputs a plan directly or generates a PDDL formulation for a classical solver. Aggressive commitment risks costly failures, while conservative commitment risks excessive LLM calls. ### 13. ToolBottleneckBench: Measuring Tool-Agent Performance under Controlled Pressures and Fixed Harness Budgets

- **arXiv ID**: [2610.21098](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.21098v1)
- **Authors**: Zichuan Xu, Yuyang Dong, Makoto Onizuka, Chuan Xiao
- **Categories**: cs.CL, cs.AI, cs.LG, cs.SE
- **Published**: 2026-10-15
- **Relevance**: title:agent, abstract:agent
- **Abstract**: The success score of a tool agent reflects both its model and the budgets of the harness that runs it. We present ToolBottleneckBench, a benchmark that measures tool-agent performance under controlled pressures and fixed harness budgets. Synthetic enterprise tasks stay fixed while one pressure varies: the registered tool catalog, the visible tool menu, the certified workflow, or the encoding of returned records. An exact grader records the first unmet obligation of each failed episode and the harness limit, if any, that ended it. Across 21 model and harness configurations, the visible menu is the only one of the three pressures that act on the model with a large penalty, and this penalty tracks truncation at the completion cap.

- **Relevance**: title:agent, abstract:agent
- **Abstract**: Tool-using language agents must revise prepared actions when evidence changes or authority is revoked. ### 15. Each Reward to Its Relevant Tokens: Reward-Specific Token-Level Credit Assignment for Multi-Reward RL

- **arXiv ID**: [2610.21439](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.21439v1)
- **Authors**: Skylar Zhai, Haonan Wang, Zhining Liu, Linxin Song, Xuwei Ding, Jingcheng Liang, Jian Kang
- **Categories**: cs.LG
- **Published**: 2026-10-15
- **Relevance**: abstract:tool calling
- **Abstract**: Language models are increasingly trained against several verifiable rewards at once, such as the correctness and the format of a tool call, or the individual constraints of a complex instruction. Reward-decoupled normalization (GDPO) normalizes each reward separately so that different reward combinations keep distinct advantages, but it still spreads every reward's advantage evenly over the whole response, although each verifier checks only a few positions. We propose MRTPO, which assigns the advantage of each reward to the tokens relevant to it, using the information its verifier checks, without auxiliary models or token-level annotations. For each reward, the policy replays its own response with and without that reward's verifier information, and the per-token Jensen-Shannon distance between the two next-token distributions measures each token's relevance and reassigns the advantage. On text-to-SQL, multi-constraint instruction following and tool calling, MRTPO converges faster and achieves better performance than the compared methods.
