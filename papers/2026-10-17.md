### 1. PROBE: Learning to Audit Policy Compliance in Tool-Using LLM Agents

- **arXiv ID**: [2610.22124](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22124v1)
- **Authors**: Kshitij Mishra, Abhijith Sharma, Nils Lukas, Salem Lahlou
- **Categories**: cs.AI
- **Published**: 2026-10-15
- **Relevance**: title:agent, title:LLM agent, title:LLM, abstract:tool use, abstract:agent, abstract:multi-turn
- **Abstract**: Modern agentic systems extend language models with external tools, enabling them to complete complex tasks through multi-turn interactions. As these agents are deployed in consequential settings, they are often constrained by policies governing pricing, privacy, confirmation, safety, and other requirements. However, existing evaluation methods leave a critical gap: capability benchmarks measure what agents can accomplish, while content-safety red-teaming measures harmful generations, but neither tests whether agents comply with predefined policies during otherwise valid tool use. Such policy violations are difficult to audit because their effects may emerge only after several turns, span multiple tool calls, or surface in downstream systems. We propose PROBE (Policy Rule Observation via Behavioral Exploration), an algorithm for training LLM-based auditors that interact with deployed agents as realistic users and probe them for policy violations through multi-turn, tool-mediated dialogue.

- **Published**: 2026-10-15
- **Relevance**: title:agent, title:LLM agent, title:LLM, abstract:tool use, abstract:agent
- **Abstract**: Large language model (LLM)-based agents and multi-agent systems increasingly tackle complex tasks through long-horizon tool use and inter-agent collaboration. However, these systems are prone to deviating from the overall goal as execution unfolds, which wastes execution resources and leads to undesired outcomes, a failure termed goal drift. In this work, we propose a steering agent that supervises execution on the fly and steers the system back on track when drift occurs. The steering agent observes the execution context, deciding when to intervene with a low-cost fast path and how to intervene with a high-cost slow path. To unlock its steering intelligence, the ability to assess goal drift and generate effective interventions, we jointly train the two paths with reinforcement learning, combining a pairwise ranking objective with group-relative policy optimization (GRPO).

### 3. Training-Free On-Policy Distillation

- **arXiv ID**: [2610.22236](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22236v1)
- **Authors**: Bangji Yang, Jiajun Fan, Zhaoyu Zhu, Hongbo Ma, Ge Liu
- **Categories**: cs.LG, cs.AI, cs.CL
- **Published**: 2026-10-15
- **Relevance**: title:on-policy distillation, title:distillation, abstract:on-policy distillation, abstract:distillation, title:on-policy
- **Abstract**: On-policy distillation consolidates specialist models into one student at the price of rollouts, teacher passes and gradient steps for every teacher. We show that the consolidation does not need training: with forward passes only, the capabilities of separately tuned specialists can be aggregated into one model that is, on average, more accurate than either specialist alone. When specialists share a base model, distillation has an exact layer-wise solution: each linear layer of the fused model must reproduce every specialist's output on the inputs that specialist receives while reading its own answers, a least-squares problem whose solution needs only the second moments of those inputs. One forward pass over a few hundred rollouts provides them. We call this training-free on-policy distillation (\method).

### 4. Waypoint-OPD: Transferring RL-Induced Policy Shifts via Gated On-Policy Distillation

- **arXiv ID**: [2610.21601](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.21601v1)
- **Authors**: Bingxi Hou, Guochao Jiang, Guofeng Quan, Weiqing Li, Wenfeng Feng, Guohua Liu, Yuewei Zhang
- **Categories**: cs.LG, cs.CL
- **Published**: 2026-10-15
- **Relevance**: title:on-policy distillation, title:distillation, abstract:on-policy distillation, abstract:distillation, title:on-policy
- **Abstract**: Policy-shift distillation reuses the difference between a teacher's log probabilities before and after reinforcement learning (RL) as supervision on student-generated trajectories. However, this endpoint policy shift cannot reveal whether local action preferences evolved consistently during teacher training. The same endpoint shift can result from early and late shifts that reinforce or partially cancel each other. We investigate whether agreement between early and late teacher policy shifts can guide selective transfer. We introduce Waypoint-guided On-Policy Distillation (Waypoint-OPD), which uses an intermediate teacher checkpoint from the same RL run to compare early and late shifts in relative action preferences at student-visited prefixes.

### 5. Does On-Policy Distillation Need to Complete Every Rollout?

- **arXiv ID**: [2610.21337](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.21337v1)
- **Authors**: Zhengxin Zhang, Maosen Tang, Ning Wang, Changhao Li, Jingren Zhou, Bo Dai, Sainyam Galhotra, Alex Townsend
  *(and 1 more)*
- **Categories**: cs.LG, cs.AI
- **Published**: 2026-10-15
- **Relevance**: title:on-policy distillation, title:distillation, abstract:on-policy distillation, abstract:distillation, title:on-policy
- **Abstract**: On-policy distillation (OPD) effectively transfers capabilities from a teacher to a student through feedback on student-generated rollouts. However, generating complete student rollouts and computing teacher feedback for every token make OPD computationally expensive. We introduce Stochastic Truncated On-Policy Distillation (STOPD), which accelerates OPD by randomly truncating student rollouts and correcting the resulting sampling bias with blockwise inverse-probability weighting (IPW). At each training step, we sample a stopping position for each rollout before generation and let the student generate until that position or natural termination. We obtain teacher feedback on every generated token and use blockwise IPW to compensate for the lower probability of retaining later tokens while controlling the weights.

### 6. Compatibility Matters: A Framework for Token-Level Teacher Selection in On-Policy Distillation

- **arXiv ID**: [2610.21234](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.21234v1)

- **Categories**: cs.LG
- **Published**: 2026-10-15
- **Relevance**: title:on-policy distillation, title:distillation, abstract:on-policy distillation, abstract:distillation, title:on-policy
- **Abstract**: On-policy distillation (OPD) has recently been introduced as a promising paradigm for continual learning, where a student policy can be supervised at the token-level. Typically, OPD is performed using a single teacher per prompt rollout (or even per training run). However, this ignores a whole other axis of flexibility that is available due to the on-policy nature - token-level teacher selection during rollouts. ### 7. Condition-Contrastive Extrapolation for On-Policy Distillation of Language Models

- **arXiv ID**: [2610.21195](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.21195v1)
- **Authors**: Wenxuan Luo, Chenxi Li, Haoyue Liu, Yongxin Guo, Xiaoying Tang
- **Categories**: cs.AI
- **Published**: 2026-10-15
- **Relevance**: title:on-policy distillation, title:distillation, abstract:on-policy distillation, abstract:distillation, title:on-policy
- **Abstract**: On-policy distillation (OPD) improves a student language model by training on its own generated trajectories with supervision from a stronger teacher. However, when conditioned on student-generated prefixes, the teacher can favor invalid continuations that violate the question's premises, causing OPD to encourage erroneous reasoning steps. As a remedy, we introduce Condition-Contrastive Extrapolated On-Policy Distillation (CCE-OPD), which makes teacher supervision sensitive to question conditions. CCE-OPD incorporates (1) a fixed-prefix comparison in which the same frozen teacher evaluates the complete question and a counterpart with a selected condition masked, and (2) a bounded log-probability contrast that reweights the teacher distribution into an auxiliary distillation target. On 200 diagnostic reasoning states, condition contrast reduces the rate of preferring invalid continuations from 27.0% to 16.0%.

### 8. Rethinking the Role of Tool Graph Memory in Multi-Turn Tool Use

- **Authors**: Uiji Hwang, Richeng Xuan, Taeuk Kim
- **Categories**: cs.CL, cs.AI
- **Published**: 2026-10-15
- **Relevance**: title:tool use, title:multi-turn, abstract:tool use, abstract:agent
- **Abstract**: Large language model (LLM) agents solve complex tasks by calling external tools over multiple turns. Recent work has proposed tool graph memory, which stores past experience as tool transitions to guide subsequent selection. However, it remains unclear whether this component consistently improves agents' tool use or undermines it. We evaluate tool graph memory on nine models and two benchmarks, finding that it seldom improves significantly over baselines, and that its gains, where present, cost over twice the LLM calls. The memory's guidance is often incorrect, and random or no guidance retains most of the gain, implying that the benefit stems from extra computation rather than the memory itself.

### 9. TRACER: Trajectory-Aware Tool Retrieval for LLM Agents

- **Published**: 2026-10-15
- **Relevance**: title:agent, title:LLM agent, title:LLM, abstract:agent
- **Abstract**: Modern agents select tools from registries, but exposing entire registries incurs high token costs and can impair selection as they grow. We present TRACER, a retrieval front-end that indexes observed tool-call sequences in a trie and ranks continuations using a learned blend of empirical and contextual priors. On a mixed retail-and-telecom $τ^2$ environment, TRACER improves GPT-5.4's tool-selection accuracy by 4.7 points while reducing input tokens per step by 80%, exposing just 5 of 128 tools. In autonomous full-turn execution, it matches GPT-5.4's full-registry task success with 61% fewer input tokens per step. Across six environments and four models, TRACER improves tool-selection accuracy over full-registry selection in 21 of 24 model-environment pairs, with average gains of 6 to 10 points; the three exceptions trail by at most 2.2.

- **Categories**: cs.CV, cs.AI
- **Published**: 2026-10-15
- **Relevance**: title:distillation, abstract:on-policy distillation, abstract:distillation, title:on-policy
- **Abstract**: Reinforcement learning (RL) can elicit specialized visual capabilities in vision-language models (VLMs), yet jointly optimizing heterogeneous objectives often introduces cross-capability gradient conflicts. Training experts independently mitigates this interference, but direct reward optimization may entangle specialized knowledge with reward-hacking biases. This paper proposes Skill-Decoupled On-Policy Expert Distillation (SCOPED), which trains capability-specific experts and extracts explicit skills through reward-guided reflection. - **Abstract**: Learning from interaction experience is essential for enabling large language models to continually improve during deployment. ### 18. ACEdit: Stable Multi-Turn Image Editing via Asymmetric Conditioning

- **arXiv ID**: [2610.22328](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22328v1)
- **Authors**: Sara Dorfman, Nir Goren, Gal Barak, Daniel Cohen-Or, Or Patashnik
- **Categories**: cs.CV, cs.AI, cs.GR
- **Published**: 2026-10-15
- **Relevance**: title:multi-turn, abstract:distillation, abstract:multi-turn
- **Abstract**: Image editing models produce impressive results for individual edits, but their quality often deteriorates over multiple editing turns. Each editing turn introduces small distribution shifts that accumulate over successive edits, creating a gap between the input distributions encountered during the editor's original training and multi-turn inference. We introduce a training scheme that makes multi-turn image editing stable under this accumulating distribution shift. A key is to force the editor to operate on its own outputs from previous editing turns during training, bringing the principle of Self-Forcing to image editing. The main challenge is that self-generated editing trajectories deviate from ground-truth trajectories, making direct supervision unreliable.
