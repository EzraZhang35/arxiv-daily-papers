### 1. Communicative On-Policy Distillation: Distilling Black-Box Teacher Capabilities through Communication for Long-Horizon Tasks

- **arXiv ID**: [2611.23673](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23673v1)
- **Authors**: Lirui Luo, Kelong Mao, Xinwei Yang, Rongqing Li, Luyu Chen, Kieran Wong, Yudong Guo, Xinrui Wang
  *(and 4 more)*
- **Categories**: cs.LG, cs.AI, cs.CL
- **Published**: 2026-11-18
- **Relevance**: title:on-policy distillation, title:distillation, title:distilling, abstract:on-policy distillation, abstract:distillation, abstract:agent
- **Abstract**: On-policy distillation (OPD) is a powerful paradigm for improving language-model agents on long-horizon tasks, yet its conventional formulation relies on teacher logits that text-only black- box models do not expose. To address this limitation, we introduce Communicative On-Policy Distillation (COPD), which uses natural-language communication as a medium for distilling a stronger model's capabilities into a smaller student. During task execution, the student au- tonomously decides when to consult a black-box teacher and formulates open-ended natural- language requests without predefined question templates or intervention schedules. Successful student-teacher interactions are retained as privileged references to supervise fresh adviser-free student rollouts through on-policy self-distillation, jointly with task-reward reinforcement learning. On CAMG-II, COPD achieves 2.46 times the gain of the strongest black-box OPD baseline.

### 2. Which Tokens, in What Order, and How Fast? A Theoretical Analysis of On-Policy Distillation Objectives

- **arXiv ID**: [2611.23397](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23397v1)
- **Authors**: Hubert Strauss, Liam Fowl, Noam Razin
- **Categories**: cs.LG, cs.AI, stat.ML
- **Published**: 2026-11-17
- **Relevance**: title:on-policy distillation, title:distillation, abstract:on-policy distillation, abstract:distillation, title:on-policy
- **Abstract**: On-policy distillation (OPD) trains a student language model to match a teacher model based on its own generations. When using OPD, one must choose whether to apply it at the sequence or token level and which divergence to minimize. However, despite the wide adoption of OPD in practice, the formal understanding of which OPD objective is appropriate for a given setting is limited. Toward addressing this gap, we theoretically analyze how the choice of OPD objective governs which tokens are prioritized, in what order, and how efficiently. Our analysis reveals that sequence-level OPD exhibits a future agreement bias, which myopic token-level objectives avoid.

### 3. Streaming On-Policy Distillation: Decoupling Three Clocks for Stage-Wise Supervision Consumption

- **arXiv ID**: [2611.22298](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22298v1)
- **Authors**: Yun Zhu Da Wang
- **Categories**: cs.AI
- **Published**: 2026-11-17
- **Relevance**: title:on-policy distillation, title:distillation, abstract:on-policy distillation, abstract:distillation, title:on-policy
- **Abstract**: In on-policy distillation (OPD), response completion, supervision delivery, and parameter updates need not share the same granularity. We develop streaming OPD by decoupling these three clocks: a response repeatedly delivers newly generated segments while generation continues, retaining the full causal prefix at each delivery and computing loss only on new positions. A consumption protocol then organizes supervision budgets, position selection, staleness handling, and update scheduling, allowing control over quantity, coverage, and version lag across training stages. In GSM8K experiments distilling Qwen3-4B into Qwen3-0.6B with VERL, supervised-budget matching reduces the early gap between best test accuracies over seven common candidates from 4.55 to 2.42 points. Synchronous continuation from the same checkpoint reaches 73.39% best test accuracy over seven common candidates at u=384,...,576, versus 74.30% synchronous and 71.95% budget-matched (differences -0.91 and -2.35 points); an unpaired extended record reaches 76.12%.

### 4. SpecLook: Speculating Where to Look for Fast and Reliable Agentic MLLMs

- **arXiv ID**: [2611.23483](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23483v1)
- **Authors**: Xi Ding, Jiawei Zhang
- **Categories**: cs.AI, cs.CL, cs.CV, cs.LG
- **Published**: 2026-11-17
- **Relevance**: title:agent, title:agentic, title:LLM, abstract:agent
- **Abstract**: Agentic multimodal large language models (MLLMs) that think with images interleave reasoning with zoom-in tool calls. However, this sequential loop is slow, and its answers carry no reliability score. Prior speculation of tokens, reasoning steps, or answers keeps the loop, and answer speculation also accepts confident errors. We find that the loop mostly adds one zoom-in step, with which a small model is ahead of the agent on most splits. Our key insight is to speculate the agent's next observation, which, unlike a drafted answer, can be verified, corrected, and used to test the answer.

### 5. SilverSpoon-KD: A General-Purpose Toolkit for Knowledge Distillation

- **arXiv ID**: [2611.22842](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22842v1)
- **Authors**: Xaver R. Davey, David Broman
- **Categories**: cs.LG
- **Published**: 2026-11-17
- **Relevance**: title:distillation, title:knowledge distillation, abstract:distillation
- **Abstract**: Knowledge distillation transfers deep learning representations from a teacher model to a student model, typically reducing model size or inference cost. However, knowledge distillation tooling has not kept pace with the breadth of research methods: existing toolkits typically support only a limited subset of techniques and use cases. We present SilverSpoon-KD, an open-source PyTorch toolkit that unifies different distillation paradigms and architectural relationships across workloads and applications behind a single API. Moreover, SilverSpoon-KD offers the first general-purpose blockwise knowledge distillation implementation with unique scalability features. We validate its versatility across vision, masked language model, and causal language models on tasks spanning homogeneous, heterogeneous, and quantization distillation across paradigms.

- **Relevance**: title:agent, abstract:agent, abstract:multi-turn
- **Abstract**: Multimodal agents largely rely on generic tools such as cropping, search, and code execution, leaving specialist vision models outside their tool space. - **Categories**: cs.CL, cs.AI
- **Published**: 2026-11-17
- **Relevance**: title:agent, abstract:agent
- **Abstract**: In repository-level question answering (QA), an agent explores a code repository with tools and should answer a question once enough information is gathered. Nevertheless, we find that LLM agents often continue to invoke tools even after the gathered information is already sufficient to answer. We call this phenomenon over-exploration. Moreover, even though existing methods improve task performance through supervised fine-tuning (SFT) on expert trajectories, SFT also propagates the over-exploration in the trajectories to the agent. ### 11. Not All Evidence Is Admissible: Evidence Governance for Tool-Augmented Medical Agents

- **arXiv ID**: [2611.22169](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22169v1)
- **Authors**: Runxun Zhang, Onofrio Antonio Catalano, Jinqiao Wang, Jingwei Wei
- **Categories**: cs.AI, cs.CV
- **Published**: 2026-11-17
- **Relevance**: title:agent, abstract:agent
- **Abstract**: Tool-augmented medical agents treat whatever their tools return as evidence. We show that this default is costly: medical tools fail silently, and a frozen reasoner cannot ignore what enters its context, so many cases it would have answered correctly are flipped to wrong once tool outputs are added. We call this evidence hijacking. $\mathcal{V}$-information explains the phenomenon: the reasoner has lost the option to ignore, so a tool output can carry negative usable information, and moving the decision of what to keep outside the reasoner restores that option. The theory further shows that part of any filtering gain comes merely from letting the reasoner see less, and only selection by content lets it see better.

- **Published**: 2026-11-17
- **Relevance**: title:LLM, abstract:distillation
- **Abstract**: Collecting large-scale, diverse, and high-quality post-training datasets for autoregressive language models is challenging. Consequently, Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) is typically applied to datasets significantly smaller than those used for pre-training, which leads to overfitting and the catastrophic forgetting of the tails of the data distribution. To prevent this collapse, we introduce Ambient SFT, a principled framework for leveraging imperfect predictions to augment the fine-tuning set. - **Categories**: cs.LG
- **Published**: 2026-11-17
- **Relevance**: title:distilling, abstract:distillation
- **Abstract**: Kernelized attention methods, which aim to replace the $O(N^2)$ complexity of softmax attention with $O(N)$ alternatives, have been the focus of intense research in the last five years. Despite growing interest, systematic evaluations and fair comparisons across approaches and scales are still missing. We analyze kernelized attention in two halves and highlight prominent limitations on each. We also discover that reported efficiency gains often fail to reproduce against modern baselines.

- **Categories**: cs.AI
- **Published**: 2026-11-17
- **Relevance**: title:agent, abstract:agent
- **Abstract**: As AI agents take on increasingly open-ended and complex tasks, verifying their outputs has proven to be difficult. Rubric-graded agent environments, where a verifier judges multiple natural-language criteria against the deliverables and environment state changes produced by the agent, have emerged as a popular paradigm. We conduct the first systematic study of verifiers for rubric-graded agent environments.
