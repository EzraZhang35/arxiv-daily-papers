### 1. Stabilizing On-Policy Targeted Guidance in Agentic Reinforcement Learning

- **arXiv ID**: [2611.05266](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05266v1)
- **Authors**: Kevin Frans, Alexander M Rush, Sergey Levine, Pieter Abbeel, Ashvin Nair
- **Categories**: cs.LG
- **Published**: 2026-11-03
- **Relevance**: title:agent, title:agentic, abstract:distillation, abstract:agent, abstract:multi-turn, title:on-policy
- **Abstract**: Naive on-policy RL is often bottlenecked by the capabilities of the base model --- insufficient coverage can result in an inability to learn from certain rewards. At the same time, rewards in modern post-training systems often come from rich textual sources, e.g. judge models with a rubric of criteria. Can we utilize such structures to provide a rich directional signal that enables improving on otherwise difficult to optimize rewards? In this work, we examine a framework we refer to as targeted guidance that learns from per-turn textual feedback via an on-policy self-distillation objective.

### 2. OperAItor: An Agentic Framework for Fault-Tolerant Operation of Chemical Processes

- **arXiv ID**: [2611.05179](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05179v1)
- **Authors**: Javal Vyas, Mehmet Mercang{ö}z
- **Categories**: eess.SY, cs.AI
- **Published**: 2026-11-03
- **Relevance**: title:agent, title:agentic, abstract:distillation, abstract:agent
- **Abstract**: OperAItor, an open-source framework for developing and evaluating large language model agents for supervisory fault management in chemical processes. The framework is demonstrated on a dynamic reactor-distillation process representing isopropanol dehydrogenation, with physically based thermodynamics and modified reaction kinetics to permit liquid-phase operation. The process is represented by a 113-variable differential-algebraic model and operates at a nominal feed rate of 100kmol/h, producing approximately 4t/h of acetone. The simulated plant includes 8 PI control loops, 22 process alarms, 5 duty-standby pump trains, and 11 independent safety instrumented functions. The agent architecture integrates multivariate statistical process monitoring, fault diagnosis, knowledge-graph retrieval, action planning, and supervisory action generation.

  *(and 7 more)*
- **Categories**: cs.AI
- **Published**: 2026-11-03
- **Relevance**: title:distillation, title:agent, abstract:distillation, abstract:agent
- **Abstract**: Multi-expert agent marketplaces -- where creators publish domain-specific AI experts for end users to consult -- are an emerging deployment pattern for language technology. A published expert can receive many requests yet little evidence about its creator, who remains active elsewhere: the cold-expert problem. We frame continuous cognitive distillation as converting attributed creator observations into revisable decision rules, communication patterns, and domain procedures. CogniLife proposes a cross-expert attribution design for this problem, with Creator Profiles for transferable creator patterns and Expert Journals for listing-specific knowledge; this design is not yet implemented or evaluated. A deployed platform partially realizes the surrounding lifecycle through creator-authorized authoring, capability-owned proposal filtering, and reversible consolidation of eligible owner-scoped memory.

### 4. MDSKILL: Learning to Internalize Skill Memory through Multi-View Distillation for Efficient Dynamic Agent Self-Evolution

- **arXiv ID**: [2611.05313](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05313v1)
- **Authors**: Dian Jin, Jinluan Yang, Kairong Han, Baohong Li, Zijing Hu, Yi He, Wenlin Zhong, Yunze Tong
  *(and 4 more)*
- **Categories**: cs.CL
- **Published**: 2026-11-03
- **Relevance**: title:distillation, title:agent, abstract:distillation, abstract:agent
- **Abstract**: Skill memories encode past experiences as textual prompts to improve reinforcement learning (RL) for self-evolving agents. Complementing RL, On-Policy Self-Distillation (OPSD) provides dense, fine-grained supervision to internalize knowledge from skill-guided trajectories and accelerate policy learning. However, self-generated memories often contain noisy supervision and functionally entangled, redundant, or conflicting knowledge, making direct distillation difficult. Under limited training budgets, these issues can hinder knowledge internalization and even degrade performance. To address this problem, we propose MDSKILL, a multi-view distillation approach for skill memory internalization.

### 6. Drift No More: Spectral Locking for Long-Horizon Open-Weight Multi-Agent AI

- **arXiv ID**: [2611.04315](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.04315v1)
- **Authors**: Vishal Pramanik, Maisha Maliha, Susmit Jha, Sumit Kumar Jha
- **Categories**: cs.AI, cs.MA
- **Published**: 2026-11-03
- **Relevance**: title:agent, abstract:tool use, abstract:agent
- **Abstract**: Long-horizon multi-agent systems suffer from three tightly coupled failures: task drift, role drift, and state staleness. We test the hypothesis that these share a common representational cause: agent hidden states move away from the directions encoding the active task, assigned role, and current shared state. A pilot study over 14,832 annotated turns supports this: drifted turns collapse near-uniformly across all three anchor subspaces (pairwise r = 0.72-0.78). Existing methods address these failures separately, operating at the surface-text level or treating control directions independently. We introduce ATLAS (Algebraic Task-role Locking with Adaptive Spectral Memory), a training-free intervention acting on hidden states before a token is generated.

### 7. AgentAM: An AI Agent for Spatter- and Defect-Aware Process Optimization in Laser Powder Bed Fusion

- **arXiv ID**: [2611.05171](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05171v1)
- **Authors**: Olabode T. Ajenifujah, Amir Barati Farimani
- **Categories**: cs.AI
- **Published**: 2026-11-03
- **Relevance**: title:agent, abstract:agent
- **Abstract**: Predictive models for laser powder bed fusion (LPBF) are rarely used on the shop floor because querying them requires programming and model-specific knowledge. We present AgentAM, a large language model (LLM) agent that gives process engineers natural-language access to CFD-trained surrogates of spatter and melt pool geometry for SS316L through the Model Context Protocol (MCP). Polynomial surrogates trained on 279 single-track simulations predict melt pool dimensions ($R^2 \geq 0.95$) and spatter volume ($R^2 = 0.83$ on test data) at any power-velocity point in the trained range, and geometric criteria assign each point a geometry-derived defect regime (keyhole, lack of fusion, balling or stable) that matches the regime obtained from the simulated geometry in 93.4% of tracks under cross-validation. The agent selects and parameterizes tools for prediction, process-window scans and constrained optimization. Minimum-spatter optima often sat within 10 $μ$m of the lack-of-fusion boundary; a margin-aware optimizer moved them away from all thresholds and raised the estimated probability of remaining classified Good from 0.94 to 1.00 under an assumed 2% process drift, and from 0.72 to 0.91 when jointly sampled cross-validated geometry residuals were added.

### 8. ToolSpace: Scaling Tool-Centric Environment Synthesis for Long-Horizon Agents

- **arXiv ID**: [2611.05036](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05036v1)

  *(and 17 more)*
- **Categories**: cs.CL
- **Published**: 2026-11-03
- **Relevance**: title:agent, abstract:agent
- **Abstract**: Large language models are deployed as long-horizon agents that compose diverse tools across MCP services into executable workflows. Existing MCP environment-scaling methods are data-centric: they first synthesize a database and scenario, then derive tools and tasks from them. As a result, tool interfaces may not match real ones, tool compositions are not grounded in execution, and tasks rarely span more than one environment. We propose ToolSpace, a tool-centric framework that reverses this order. ### 9. TactiNavi: Serving and Revising an LLM Game Coach with Explicit Strategy Conditions

- **arXiv ID**: [2611.04772](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.04772v1)
- **Authors**: Qi Liu, Xiaoyang Yuan, Zhuomeng Zhang, Yubin Ruan, Xinyi Mou, Wenjin Wang, Hanying Jiao, Di Wu
  *(and 4 more)*
- **Categories**: cs.AI
- **Published**: 2026-11-03
- **Relevance**: title:LLM, abstract:agent
- **Abstract**: A deployed LLM coaching agent answers requests whose correct response depends on player state and game version, so advice that is right for one player can be wrong for the next. We describe TactiNavi, the serving system behind the LLM coach of a commercial open-world game, which makes strategy applicability an explicit artifact shared by offline compilation, online execution, failure diagnosis, and release. An offline compiler turns versioned game evidence and expert guidance into strategy packages with explicit prerequisites, the serving plane evaluates those prerequisites as three-valued conditions over structured player state and records decision traces, and a control plane uses the resulting condition-rule dependencies to validate revisions on held-out traffic before release. In a retrospective evaluation of 1,122 logged coaching turns from three deployment periods, condition-bearing packages raise correctness from 57.9% to 82.0% over atomic facts and reduce strategy misapplication under changed player state from 18.1% to 10.4%, while trace-based claim verification reaches 86.4% precision at a median reply latency of 2.07 s. The deployment also showed that most apparent fixes are generation noise, because an unchanged-system rerun recovers 38.4% of failing turns and only one of 15 shipped revision units passes the release checks retrospectively.

### 10. From Watching to Steering: Enabling Human-Coding Agent Collaboration through Execution Traces

- **Categories**: cs.HC, cs.AI
- **Published**: 2026-11-03
- **Relevance**: title:agent, abstract:agent
- **Abstract**: Recent advances in large language models (LLMs) have enabled coding agents to autonomously perform complex software development tasks, yet their long-running execution remains difficult for users to inspect, understand, and steer. We present Vibetrace, an interactive trace visualization and intervention system for agentic programming built upon OpenCode. Vibetrace introduces a human-agent collaboration paradigm of Observe, Understand, Intervene, and Evolve. It (1) visualizes agent workflows as interactive execution traces for inspecting actions, intermediate states, and failures; (2) enables users to fork execution from prior steps and provide targeted corrections without restarting validated progress; and (3) distills traces and human feedback into reusable skills for continual agent evolution. On SWE-bench Verified, distilled skills reduce input tokens by 23.0% and cost by 15.5%, while improving pass rate from 65.0% to 68.0%.

### 11. Patching the Protocol: A Taxonomy of Pre-Registration Repairs in LLM-Proposer Experiments

- **arXiv ID**: [2611.04338](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.04338v1)
- **Authors**: Peter Chen, Ni Zhang, Siyuan Zhang
- **Categories**: cs.AI, cs.SE
- **Published**: 2026-11-03
- **Relevance**: title:LLM
- **Abstract**: Pre-registration is increasingly proposed as a trust mechanism for machine-learning research involving LLM-generated content, but its canonical form assumes a frozen protocol can be executed unchanged. Software-intensive studies violate this assumption: the protocol is partly implemented, and implementations carry defects. We report a three-case study of protocol repairs ("patches") from a program where an LLM proposes executable rules that a programmatic verifier gates, across production scheduling, incentive design, and task assignment. Each protocol required exactly one defect-repair patch: three events in total. None modified a numbered judgment criterion, its threshold, or the scoring rule, and none altered already collected data; two changed pre-registered design parameters before any data were collected, each with a recorded rationale.

### 12. Sketchpad: certified exact arithmetic with a small vision-language model

- **arXiv ID**: [2611.04434](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.04434v1)
- **Authors**: Mostafa 'Neo' Mohsenvand
- **Categories**: cs.LG, cs.CL, cs.LO
- **Published**: 2026-11-03
- **Relevance**: abstract:tool use
- **Abstract**: Language models are accurate on most long-arithmetic problems, but their accuracy is estimated on samples and carries no guarantee for an unseen input; tool use is exact but moves the computation outside the network. We show that exactness at all input lengths becomes a finite, checkable property of a network when each of its steps reads only a bounded view of an external working memory. In Sketchpad, Qwen3.5-0.8B with a low-rank adapter executes arithmetic procedures on a rendered sheet. Each forward pass reads a three-cell view and emits one step: a digit, a carry or borrow, and the next operation. A fixed runtime moves the view and writes no digit of a result.

### 13. A research world model of power engineering learns its dynamics and reveals what-ifs

- **arXiv ID**: [2611.04566](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.04566v1)
- **Authors**: Fanping Sui, Ying Zhang, Liwei Lin
- **Categories**: cs.AI, eess.SY
- **Published**: 2026-11-03
- **Abstract**: Decisions about the direction of research in power engineering determine which technologies mature and, through them, shape infrastructure that lasts decades, yet their consequences cannot be observed before they are taken, and existing forecasts of research fields extrapolate a single trajectory. Here we introduce research world models, which treat a research field as a dynamical system that can be acted upon, and establish one for power engineering from 1.1 million papers. A distilled language model reads each paper into role-typed concepts, two sophisticated, high-fidelity graph-temporal neural network cores learn how the resulting concept network evolves, and operators learned from natural experiments in the publication record estimate the effects of interventions. In temporally held-out tests, the model forecast years ahead which concepts would grow and which method-target pairs would be joined for the first time, outperforming tuned machine-learning baselines in most settings, and its estimated intervention effects tracked those realized in natural experiments it had not seen. Its training scheme and three of its operators replicated in two fields withheld from development.

### 14. Where Did the Update Go? Certifying Declared Change Envelopes for Model Updates

- **arXiv ID**: [2611.04192](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.04192v1)
- **Authors**: Muhammad Usama, Muhammad Saad Qadir
- **Categories**: cs.LG
- **Published**: 2026-11-03
- **Abstract**: Deployed language models are updated continually, and regulators increasingly accept changes that were declared in advance. Showing that an update changed behavior only where it was declared to is a different statistical problem from bounding how much it changed: a useful update is meant to change some decisions, so global disagreement and regression bounds cannot certify it. We formalize the problem with declared change envelopes. Before training, the developer commits to a region of inputs, and every decision that changes outside it counts as leakage. Leakage compares two models, so it can be certified from unlabeled traffic with anytime-valid tests whose sample cost we bound from above and below, and per-update certificates compose into a bound on lifetime drift outside all declared scopes.
