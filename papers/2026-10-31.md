### 1. ATLAS-Bench: An Agentic Tool-Use Benchmark for Local Area Search

- **arXiv ID**: [2610.39438](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.39438v1)
- **Authors**: Keyun Zhang, Dapeng Yin, Fanfu Wang, Shuang Lang, Zhenyu Zhu, Yulin Huang, Chong Yang, Pengjie Wang
- **Categories**: cs.AI
- **Published**: 2026-10-29
- **Relevance**: title:tool-use, title:agent, title:agentic, abstract:agent
- **Abstract**: Reliable local search is essential for LLM agents to support everyday decisions and longer plans by finding places that meet users' needs at given times and locations. Yet existing benchmarks provide limited coverage of whether search results satisfy mandatory requirements, account for preferences, and ground factual and experiential judgments in place attributes and reviews. We introduce ATLAS-Bench, a benchmark of 1,200 queries across three regions, expanded from real search events using place attributes, reviews, and existing user profiles from the platform. Each query distinguishes mandatory requirements from preferences and factual conditions from subjective judgments, with supporting evidence and criteria for checking each requirement. Queries are validated through iterative assertion checks and cross-model review.

### 2. AgentDecept: Benchmarking Deceptive Behavior in Tool-Using LLM Agents under Pressure

- **arXiv ID**: [2610.39377](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.39377v1)
- **Authors**: Yehan Zhang, Qianqi Yan, Hang Yin, Ching-Chen Kuo, Shan Jiang, Ming Ouyang, Yijie Liu, Xin Eric Wang
- **Categories**: cs.AI
- **Published**: 2026-10-29
- **Relevance**: title:agent, title:LLM agent, title:LLM, abstract:agent, abstract:multi-turn
- **Abstract**: LLM agents increasingly carry out multi-turn tasks across everyday applications, yet users often see only the agent's report rather than the observations, API calls, and state changes behind it. Existing deception benchmarks have studied long-horizon interactions and tool-use settings, but whether agents faithfully report their actions across realistic, stateful application workflows remains underexplored. To address this gap, we introduce AgentDecept, an executable benchmark for evaluating behavioral deception under pressure. AgentDecept contains 477 pressure variants derived from 257 source tasks across six pressure sources, each paired with a pressure-free counterpart. Interventions enter through event messages, task context, application state, tool responses, and service quotas, spanning explicit events and implicit task or environment changes.

### 3. Turing LLM Post-Training: Calibrated Distillation and Efficient Dynamic-MoE Execution

- **arXiv ID**: [2610.39876](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.39876v1)
- **Authors**: Yue He, Jiahui Hu, Shaman Tang, Hua Zhou, Qiman Wu, Menglin Li, Yizhao Wang, Yuheng Zhang
  *(and 14 more)*
- **Categories**: cs.LG, cs.AI
- **Published**: 2026-10-29
- **Relevance**: title:distillation, title:LLM, abstract:on-policy distillation, abstract:distillation
- **Abstract**: We study post-training for dynamically routed mixture-of-experts (MoE) models, focusing on integrating independently optimized specialists while keeping consolidation efficient. Our approach combines calibrated multi-teacher on-policy distillation (C-MOPD), which matches each selected teacher on a local Top-$K$ support with teacher-wise loss normalization and clipping, with an execution path that uses post-capacity bounded-width dispatch, final-route replay, and asynchronous rollout--training overlap. On Turing-20B-A2B, the selected model achieves a four-domain normalized integration score of 0.9988 and retains at least 90.16\% of the measured specialist gain in every domain. At matched $K=32$, the full calibration objective scores 0.9962, compared with 0.9724 without calibration and 0.9834 without clipping. At a matched 8K response cap, the model exceeds both shared SFT and Qwen3-8B on all four reasoning-enabled mathematics and code benchmarks (unweighted mean: 85.92, versus 80.33 for Qwen3-8B and 74.21 for SFT), and improves over SFT on ten of twelve broader evaluation metrics.

### 7. Prefix Executability: Evaluating Tool-Using Agents Beyond Final Success

- **arXiv ID**: [2610.40444](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40444v1)
- **Authors**: Amir H. Rezaeian, Weiyi Sun, Yassine Benajiba, Dan Roth
- **Categories**: cs.AI, cs.CL
- **Published**: 2026-10-29
- **Relevance**: title:agent, abstract:agent, abstract:multi-turn
- **Abstract**: Tool-use benchmarks often score agents by final task success, but terminal outcomes can obscure whether the intermediate tool-call trajectory was executable. We propose prefix executability as a trajectory-level evaluation protocol for tool-using agents. The protocol measures whether every prefix of a predicted tool-call sequence remains executable under tool schemas, state preconditions, and data dependencies, and summarizes reliability using Prefix Executability Curves (PEC), First-Error Depth (FED), Trajectory Executability Rate (TER), horizon-normalized AUC-PEC, and Recovery Rate (RR). We instantiate the protocol on stratified samples from BFCL-v3 multi-turn and ToolSandbox, and on the complete Airline/Retail base splits of $τ^3$-bench. We observe that outcome-only and prefix-level rankings diverge: agents whose execution is conditioned on an unverified plan often achieve higher point-estimate final success than non-plan-conditioned baselines while substantially lowering TER and AUC-PEC.

### 8. Locating and Closing Information Gaps in Multi-Agent Systems

- **arXiv ID**: [2610.40186](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40186v1)
- **Authors**: Yaxin Gong, Chongming Gao, Gangyi Zhang, Chenxiao Fan, Leyang Shen, Jiakai Wang, Dong Wang, Yang Liu
  *(and 2 more)*
- **Categories**: cs.AI
- **Published**: 2026-10-29
- **Relevance**: title:agent, abstract:tool use, abstract:agent
- **Abstract**: Multi-step Web tasks often require several LLM agents to gather and combine evidence. The communication graph of a fixed workflow restricts which agents can read each other's outputs: evidence retrieved by one agent may never reach the agent that needs it, leaving an information gap and an incomplete or incorrect answer. Adding a connection can deliver the missing evidence, but can also lead the receiver to adopt an incorrect upstream conclusion that displaces a correct answer; the final output alone does not separate the two. We propose Selective Probing and Link Insertion for Closing information gaps in multi-agent Execution (SPLICE), which locates information gaps per query and decides what to adopt after adding an edge. After the original workflow runs, SPLICE uses the query and node outputs to locate a possible gap, adds one directed edge from a source to a receiver to deliver the relevant evidence, and re-executes the receiver and affected downstream nodes, producing a candidate answer.

### 9. Every Token Needs Its Own Teacher: Multi-teacher Adaptive On-policy Self-distillation

- **arXiv ID**: [2610.40574](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40574v1)
- **Authors**: Yifu Luo, Chuang Ma, Zeyu Chen, Enkelejda Kasneci, Qi Chen, Gjergji Kasneci
- **Categories**: cs.CL
- **Published**: 2026-10-29
- **Relevance**: title:distillation, abstract:distillation, title:on-policy
- **Abstract**: On-policy Self-distillation (OPSD) has proven effective for post-training large language models by constructing a teacher conditioned on privileged references. However, existing approaches keep the teacher fixed either throughout training by consistently exposing it to the full reference, or within each response by adaptively varying partial reference exposure only across responses. In this paper, we argue that neither of them is sufficient. Instead, every token needs its own teacher, as the teacher preference can vary token by token within the same response. Through a teacher-intervention pilot study, we validate that the teacher preference differs across tokens, and reveal that a preferred teacher can be identified for each token through majority voting and KL-based selection.

### 11. Does Scaling Toward AGI Depend on What System-1 Lets System-2 Execute?

- **arXiv ID**: [2610.39497](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.39497v1)
- **Authors**: Qi Wang, Yun Qu, Lizhou Cai, Heming Zou, Haoang Chi, Yiqin Lv, Xiang Li, Haiyan Feng
  *(and 5 more)*
- **Categories**: cs.AI
- **Published**: 2026-10-29
- **Relevance**: abstract:tool use, abstract:agent
- **Abstract**: Progress toward artificial general intelligence (AGI) has largely relied on scaling a resource-intensive executive, System-2, through larger models, more data and compute, longer reasoning, and more agents. We ask whether the next stage may come instead from co-designing it with System-1, a lightweight, semantically aware predictor that evaluates candidate interactions before execution, so that System-2 acts only on those worth their cost. Our answer is a qualified yes. We study \textit{conditional interaction}, a predict--gate--execute--update loop: System-1 estimates each candidate's utility, System-2 executes those admitted by an adaptive threshold, and the outcomes update System-1. The loop spans training, test-time reasoning, adaptive decision-making, and agentic tool use, with utility and cost defined by the application.

### 12. InferD: A Honeypot Framework for AI Inference Infrastructure

- **arXiv ID**: [2610.39807](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.39807v1)
- **Authors**: Hicham Hammouchi, Gabriele Lenzini
- **Categories**: cs.CR, cs.AI
- **Published**: 2026-10-29
- **Relevance**: abstract:agent
- **Abstract**: Large language model serving stacks, commercial inference APIs, vector databases, and agent orchestration gateways are now ex- posed on the public internet and indexed by commodity scanners within hours of deployment. The threat characteristics of this sur- face are only beginning to be measured, and the honeypot frame- works that could measure it were designed for conventional net- work services rather than for AI infrastructure. In fact, emulating an AI service is a distinct deception problem: the observable behavior of the service, including model inventories, token formats, tool-call protocols, and authorization flows, must be reproduced faithfully, while the underlying computation, model inference and tool ex- ecution, must never occur. We present InferD, a low-interaction honeypot framework organized around a single principle we call deception without inference: every request an adversary can observe is answered from static response pools, and nothing an adversary submits is ever executed. InferD decomposes the emulation into four deception planes, transport, service protocol, vulnerability surface, and agentic session, adds a provenance plane that links a planted credential to evidence of downstream model-mediated processing.

### 13. Latent Path Exploration via Potential-Guided Subtrajectory Balance

- **Authors**: Xiaoling Zeng, Qi Zhang
- **Categories**: cs.CL, cs.LG
- **Published**: 2026-10-29
- **Relevance**: abstract:distillation
- **Abstract**: Continuous latent reasoning enables language models to perform intermediate computation without generating explicit reasoning traces. Advances in stochastic latent reasoning allow multiple paths to be sampled in continuous space, but stochasticity alone does not ensure effective exploration. A key challenge is guiding stochastic transitions between intermediate latent states toward useful reasoning paths. We introduce SubLAT, which couples a relative answer potential with Subtrajectory Balance to learn stochastic latent exploration. The relative potential evaluates the answer support of sampled states against replacement states, with a correctness signal at the terminal state.

### 14. Aligning Medical Vision Language Models at the Capability Boundary with Entropy Adaptive Rubric Grounding

- **Authors**: Hongyu Bai, Jiaqi Zeng, Chunzheng Zhu
- **Categories**: cs.CV, cs.AI
- **Published**: 2026-10-29
- **Abstract**: Medical diagnostic reasoning requires verifiable links between visual evidence and clinical knowledge, yet current medical vision language models are aligned with objectives that reward the wrong behavior. Uniform supervised fine tuning lets easy cases dominate updates while clinically critical boundary cases saturate prematurely. Sparse rewards based only on final-answer correctness cannot assess anatomical grounding or clinical logic, inviting reward hacking through answer shortcuts. We propose MedCBR, a two-stage alignment framework coupling boundary-aware preference optimization with rubric-grounded reinforcement learning. First, we construct counterfactual boundary preferences at the model's capability frontier using empirical pass rates and preference pairs that share reasoning prefixes but diverge at decisive diagnostic branches.
