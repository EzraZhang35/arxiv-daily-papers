### 1. Communicative On-Policy Distillation: Distilling Black-Box Teacher Capabilities through Communication for Long-Horizon Tasks

- **arXiv ID**: [2611.23673](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23673v1)
- **Authors**: Lirui Luo, Kelong Mao, Xinwei Yang, Rongqing Li, Luyu Chen, Kieran Wong, Yudong Guo, Xinrui Wang
  *(and 4 more)*
- **Categories**: cs.LG, cs.AI, cs.CL
- **Published**: 2026-11-18
- **Relevance**: title:on-policy distillation, title:distillation, title:distilling, abstract:on-policy distillation, abstract:distillation, abstract:agent
- **Abstract**: On-policy distillation (OPD) is a powerful paradigm for improving language-model agents on long-horizon tasks, yet its conventional formulation relies on teacher logits that text-only black- box models do not expose. To address this limitation, we introduce Communicative On-Policy Distillation (COPD), which uses natural-language communication as a medium for distilling a stronger model's capabilities into a smaller student. During task execution, the student au- tonomously decides when to consult a black-box teacher and formulates open-ended natural- language requests without predefined question templates or intervention schedules. Successful student-teacher interactions are retained as privileged references to supervise fresh adviser-free student rollouts through on-policy self-distillation, jointly with task-reward reinforcement learning. On CAMG-II, COPD achieves 2.46 times the gain of the strongest black-box OPD baseline.

### 11. Replay, Don't Resample: Off-Policy Consolidation of Long-Horizon RL Experts

- **arXiv ID**: [2611.24059](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24059v1)
- **Authors**: Zhenrui Yue, Gongxiaohui Chen, Huimin Zeng, Honglei Zhuang, Jiashuo Sun, Zhen Han, Dong Wang
- **Categories**: cs.LG, cs.AI
- **Published**: 2026-11-18
- **Relevance**: abstract:on-policy distillation, abstract:distillation, abstract:agent
- **Abstract**: Frontier post-training follows a common recipe: train one expert per domain with reinforcement learning (RL), then consolidate the experts into one model via multi-teacher on-policy distillation (MOPD). Such consolidation is expensive: the student samples in every environment again, an expert scores every sampled token, and the student inherits the drift of the final experts. Yet we argue that the experts' RL runs have already produced what consolidation needs: rollouts with their sampling probabilities and rewards, in the order the experts learned from them. We propose multi-expert replay policy distillation (MRPD), which replays these training logs through the student in the experts' order. At each step, the policy gradient is recomputed on these scored trajectories, and the final experts' distributions are distilled at the logged prefixes.

### 18. Compression Effects on Low-Resource Multilingual Translation

- **arXiv ID**: [2611.24043](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24043v1)
- **Authors**: Abbas Sani
- **Categories**: cs.CL
- **Published**: 2026-11-18
- **Abstract**: We present a pilot study characterising the effect of post-training quantisation on multilingual machine translation quality and inference cost for three African languages - Hausa, Swahili, and Yoruba - across three model families: a small general-purpose LLM (Qwen2.5-1.5B), a larger multilingual instruction-tuned LLM (Aya-23-8B), and a dedicated multilingual MT model (NLLB-200-distilled-1.3B). Evaluating on the FLORES-200 devtest set (1,012 sentences per direction) under FP16, bitsandbytes INT8, and NF4 INT4 precisions on consumer-grade NVIDIA T4 GPUs, we find: (i) the specialised MT model outperforms both LLMs by 32.7-43.3 chrF on the Hausa and Swahili directions, and by 12.9-24.7 chrF on Yoruba, despite being roughly a sixth the size of the 8B instruction-tuned LLM; (ii) post-training quantisation preserves aggregate translation quality within approximately 1 chrF across all model families and language directions, with a single worst case of 2.19 chrF; (iii) on consumer GPUs, INT8 and INT4 quantisation provide substantial memory savings (49-61% at INT4) but do not reduce inference latency relative to FP16 - in fact, latency increases by 1.3x to 5.3x; (iv) a clear performance gradient tracking training-resource availability is visible across languages (Swahili > Hausa > Yoruba). These findings motivate a research agenda focused on hardware-aware compression and error-type analysis beyond aggregate metrics for low-resource deployment.
