### 1. Learning Dynamics of On-Policy Distillation

- **arXiv ID**: [2611.08016](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08016v1)
- **Authors**: Guan Zhe Hong, Sumeet Ramesh Motwani, Liang Chen, Yangchen Pan, Junchi Yu, Jialin Yu, Philip Torr, Yi Ren
- **Categories**: cs.LG, cs.CL
- **Published**: 2026-11-05
- **Relevance**: title:on-policy distillation, title:distillation, abstract:on-policy distillation, abstract:distillation, title:on-policy
- **Abstract**: On-policy distillation (OPD) is emerging as an important paradigm for language-model post-training, combining dense teacher supervision with training on student-generated trajectories. This creates a feedback loop where each update changes the student and thereby the training distribution that generates subsequent updates. Yet, how this feedback shapes learning and generalization remains poorly understood. We conduct a mechanistic analysis of OPD's learning dynamics, distinguishing the two feedback loop stages: conversion of the distillation signal into parameter updates, and broadcast through which these updates change predictions across prefixes and reshape the rollout distribution. This framework helps us understand both limited student responsiveness despite substantial teacher-student disagreement, and degeneration when updates that improve local teacher agreement also increase the probability of unsuccessful continuations, exposing the student to unreliable supervision in subsequent rollouts.

### 2. CoRD: Coupled Rollouts Fill the Blind Spot of On-Policy Distillation

- **arXiv ID**: [2611.07959](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07959v1)
- **Authors**: Guhan Chen, Bohan Li, Jun Rao, Hejin Wang, Songtao Tian, Yexin Xie, Xiaojun Meng, Peng Yuan
  *(and 3 more)*
- **Categories**: cs.LG, cs.CL
- **Published**: 2026-11-05
- **Relevance**: title:on-policy distillation, title:distillation, abstract:on-policy distillation, abstract:distillation, title:on-policy
- **Abstract**: On-policy distillation trains a student on its own rollouts, so states the teacher would reach but the student does not are seldom supervised. We introduce CoRD (coupled-rollout distillation), which keeps the on-policy loss and moves only the distribution of training states toward the teacher, drawing rollouts from a geometric interpolation of student and teacher through the maximal coupling of speculative decoding. The coupling keeps the student's token wherever the two distributions agree and otherwise substitutes a token from the part of the target the student underweights, so interventions are as rare as any exact sampler allows and recede as the student learns. Across six base students from two model families (Qwen3 and Qwen3.5, 0.6B to 4B), CoRD raises average pass@1 over on-policy distillation by 0.9 to 2.0 points and pass@8 by 1.2 to 3.4 points on five public math benchmarks at equal steps and data. On frozen teacher-reached states, a 4B student trained with CoRD reaches in 16 steps a gap closure that on-policy distillation does not reach in 80, and substitution rules intervening at least as often in other directions fall short of CoRD.

### 3. O$^2$PD: Overlap-Gated On-Policy Distillation on Reliable Prefix

- **arXiv ID**: [2611.07731](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07731v1)
- **Authors**: Shuaishuai Zu, Jihao Zhao, Pengfei Li, Guanghuan Fang, Ben Feng, Yuhan Liu, Biao Qin, Baochuan Yang
- **Categories**: cs.CL, cs.AI
- **Published**: 2026-11-05
- **Relevance**: title:on-policy distillation, title:distillation, abstract:on-policy distillation, abstract:distillation, title:on-policy
- **Abstract**: On-policy distillation (OPD) supplies dense token-level supervision by aligning a student to the teacher's next-token distribution on its own generated rollouts. Existing works mostly implement top-$k$ OPD, applying reverse Kullback-Leibler ($\RKL$) divergence between the teacher's and the student's renormalized distributions over teacher top-$k$ support. However, the teacher scores student-generated rollouts that are inherently off-policy for itself, leaving the reliability of its next-token guidance unclear. Restricting the divergence to the teacher top-$k$ support also discards the remaining mass outside that support. These gaps leave two questions implicit: 1) which tokens to align, and 2) how to align those tokens.

### 4. Reinforcing What the Student Has Learned: Teacher-Guided On-Policy Distillation

- **arXiv ID**: [2611.07722](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07722v1)
- **Authors**: Bozhen Fang, Linhao Zhong, Xuehao Wang, Huanyi Zheng, Hao Chen, Chunhua Shen
- **Categories**: cs.LG
- **Published**: 2026-11-05
- **Relevance**: title:on-policy distillation, title:distillation, abstract:on-policy distillation, abstract:distillation, title:on-policy
- **Abstract**: On-policy distillation (OPD) improves language-model reasoning through dense teacher feedback on student-generated responses.We first observe an amplitude disparity in this feedback: on math reasoning responses, style-related positions exhibit 3.5 times the mean absolute teacher--student log-probability difference of mathematical positions.Scaling OPD updates by these discrepancies thus directs much of the update strength toward matching the teacher's expression. We instead take the student's own pretrained base as a reference: the student--base difference records the token preferences acquired during post-training and provides supervision that does not depend on the student's distance from the teacher. Building on this reference, we propose Student-Delta On-Policy Distillation (SD-OPD), which uses centered student--base differences as update coefficients, keeps each coefficient only when its sign agrees with centered teacher--student feedback, and recomputes the student signal every training round. Across 5 teacher--student configurations with Qwen3 students of 1.7B to 8B parameters, SD-OPD improves six-task mathematics average pass@1 (avg@16) over OPD by 7.76 to 10.37 percentage points. Code is available at https://github.com/aim-uofa/sd-opd.

### 5. SKIM: Pruning Large Language Model Agents via Selective Knowledge Informed Masking

- **arXiv ID**: [2611.07558](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07558v1)
- **Authors**: Moonseok Choi, Jongwon Jeong, Minki Kang, Giung Nam, Juho Lee
- **Categories**: cs.CL, cs.AI
- **Published**: 2026-11-05
- **Relevance**: title:agent, title:large language model, abstract:on-policy distillation, abstract:distillation, abstract:agent
- **Abstract**: Large Language Model (LLM) agents are increasingly deployed under structured generation, where grammar constraints enforce output format at inference time. Whereas existing compression methods target unconstrained generation, we first identify a fundamental mismatch: under structured generation, grammar constraints reduce the model's output distribution to valid tokens, and the logits matter most at the small set of positions that decide the agent's action. Motivated by this finding, we propose SKIM (Selective Knowledge-Informed Masking), the first pruning and distillation framework for LLM agents under structured generation, with both stages guided by token role. SKIM partitions token positions into context, decision, and format categories, and this partition drives two complementary axes: (1) category-aware pruning saliency scores that preserve parameters most relevant for decision positions; and (2) an on-policy distillation objective that aligns student and teacher on valid tokens, paired with category-aware feature matching. We evaluate SKIM on tool-calling benchmarks and within multi-step agent loops, across multiple model scales and sparsity levels.

### 9. Seeing Beyond the Next Step: World-Model-Guided Human-Like Navigation in Multi-Agent Scenes

- **arXiv ID**: [2611.07730](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07730v1)
- **Authors**: Seong-Eun Hong, JuYeong Hwang, Jinhyun Kim, Hanyoung Jang, HyeongYeop Kang
- **Categories**: cs.GR, cs.AI, cs.CV, cs.RO
- **Published**: 2026-11-05
- **Relevance**: title:agent, abstract:distillation, abstract:agent
- **Abstract**: Selecting a next motion in a populated scene is not just a matter of avoiding what is currently in the way: an action can be locally feasible yet drift onto the road instead of the crosswalk, cut into a stream of crossing pedestrians, or bottleneck a narrow passage. These failure modes are invisible from the current state alone, and acting on them requires reasoning about what each candidate action will cause. World models offer exactly this, namely action-conditioned predictions of the future, but rolling them out at every decision step is a cost deployable navigation systems cannot pay, and the field has largely traded foresight for speed. We argue this is a false dichotomy. The natural place for a world model in human-aware navigation is at training time, as a preference oracle that distills foresight into a fast policy, rather than at inference time, as an online planner.

- **Published**: 2026-11-05
- **Relevance**: abstract:on-policy distillation, abstract:distillation
- **Abstract**: On-policy distillation (OPD) trains a student on its own rollouts under a frozen teacher's per-token supervision. Does this dense signal reduce the training FLOPs needed to reach a target accuracy compared with reinforcement learning? We systematically compare vanilla reverse-KL OPD with Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) across task difficulty, student and teacher size, domain, and optimization choices, measuring held-out accuracy against training FLOPs. With an external post-trained teacher, OPD generally loses to GRPO in accuracy per training FLOP at matched settings, even when the teacher is further trained with GRPO, and often suffers response-length inflation. ### 18. Beyond Instruction Override: Benchmarking Indirect Prompt Injection with Task-Tampering Attacks

- **Published**: 2026-11-05
- **Relevance**: abstract:agent
- **Abstract**: Indirect prompt-injection (IPI) benchmarks predominantly test planted instructions that redirect a tool-using agent toward an attacker-chosen task. Yet attacker-controlled content can instead tamper with the outcome of the task already in progress.
