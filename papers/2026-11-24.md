### 1. Verifiable On-Policy Distillation

- **arXiv ID**: [2611.30325](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.30325v1)
- **Authors**: Juntong Shi, Stefano Ermon, Jure Leskovec, Minkai Xu
- **Categories**: cs.LG
- **Published**: 2026-11-23
- **Relevance**: title:on-policy distillation, title:distillation, abstract:on-policy distillation, abstract:distillation, title:on-policy
- **Abstract**: Reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR) has become a powerful approach for improving language-model reasoning, but its supervision is inherently sparse: a sequence-level verifier reward is propagated only through sampled trajectories and provides no direct signal for alternative actions. On-policy distillation (OPD) offers a complementary form of supervision by providing dense teacher feedback over the full next-token distribution, but this signal is not grounded in verified task outcomes. Simply co-optimizing the two objectives does not couple these signals: verifier feedback remains sparse and does not inform the dense teacher supervision. We introduce Verifiable On-Policy Distillation (VOPD), which transfers sparse verifier feedback into dense, verifier-grounded supervision. Our key observation is that the implicit-reward view of OPD places teacher supervision and verifier feedback in a common reward space while retaining a reward signal available for every next-token action.

- **Relevance**: title:agent, title:agentic, abstract:tool use, abstract:agent
- **Abstract**: Harnesses secure Computer-Using Agents by enforcing security constraints and defending against prompt injection attacks. However, we find that two core security mechanisms of agent harnesses remain vulnerable. First, consistency checking prevents agents from deviating from legitimate user objectives, while attackers can bypass it by embedding malicious actions that appear logically consistent with legitimate task execution. Second, risk auditing proactively examines high-risk operations. Yet, the auditing process itself would introduce additional exploitable actions that violate security boundaries.

### 3. Attributing Security Outcomes in LLM-Agent Evaluations: The Role of Content-Free State

- **arXiv ID**: [2611.30259](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.30259v1)
- **Authors**: Abdelmonaim Bounite, Zangir Iklassov
- **Categories**: cs.CR, cs.AI, cs.LG
- **Published**: 2026-11-23
- **Relevance**: title:agent, title:LLM, abstract:agent
- **Abstract**: Security benchmarks for LLM agents record whether each attack succeeded and compare agents, attacks, and defenses by attack success rate (ASR). Such comparisons are valid only if the agent, the attack, and the defense cause each verdict. We show that verdicts also depend on content-free state: details of a run that carry no information about the system under test, such as the Python hash seed, the order of tools or evidence, and the scoring of failed runs. We require that this state influence no verdict (decision noninterference) and treat it as untrusted input, because evaluators and requesters of LLM-based gates can choose it. Our framework, TARE (Testing Attribution of Reported Effects), attributes a reported effect to the agent, attack, or defense only if it exceeds the measured influence of content-free state.

### 4. Reasoning Beyond Imitation: Observation Conditioned Distillation for Video LLMs

- **arXiv ID**: [2611.29728](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.29728v1)
- **Authors**: Junzhou Chen, Jindong Wang, Gang Zhou
- **Categories**: cs.CV, cs.LG
- **Published**: 2026-11-22
- **Relevance**: title:distillation, title:LLM, abstract:distillation
- **Abstract**: Reasoning distillation offers a promising approach to transfer temporal reasoning capabilities of powerful Video-LLMs to open-source student models. However, teacher and student Video-LLMs may construct different temporal observations from the same video due to the adoption of distinct video sampling pipelines. A reasoning trajectory valid under the teacher's observations may rely on evidence inaccessible to the student and thus cannot be directly executed based on the student's observations. We formalize this as a conditional target mismatch, thereby revealing the Reasoning Acquisition-Execution Gap. To bridge this gap, we propose an acquisition-to-execution framework that first distills teacher trajectories into a student reasoning prior and then further optimizes the execution capability of this prior across multiple observations.

  *(and 2 more)*
- **Categories**: cs.AI
- **Published**: 2026-11-22
- **Relevance**: title:agent, abstract:agent
- **Abstract**: Large language model agents accomplish complex tasks by coordinating multiple reasoning steps and tool operations, which form dependencies among their operations. Existing orchestration methods primarily consider hard dependencies that determine when an operation can legally begin, but overlook soft dependencies, i.e., information that is not required for execution but may improve an already executable operation. We identify soft-dependency scheduling as an important problem in agent execution. To this end, we introduce Neural Agent Compiler (NAC), a learned scheduler that predicts the task-quality and resource consequences of three execution modes and selects the highest-utility legal mode at a scheduling boundary. - **Abstract**: Many large language models (LLMs) use chain-of-thought (CoT) to solve complex problems, but hide their CoT traces to prevent reasoning capabilities from being distilled. ### 13. When Can a Rewiring Control Test LLM Weight Readers? C2 and an Eligibility Protocol

- **arXiv ID**: [2611.29120](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.29120v1)
- **Authors**: Vaishak Girish Kumar, Sanika Vilas Najan
- **Categories**: cs.LG, cs.CL
- **Published**: 2026-11-22
- **Relevance**: title:LLM
- **Abstract**: Large language models can predict the outputs of small neural networks from raw weights, but success could reflect recognition of weight-space regularities (fingerprints) rather than computation, and function-preserving controls cannot tell these apart. We propose C2, a counterfactual that permutes the columns of a network's final linear readout: every other parameter, and the readout's singular values, norms and entry multiset, are unchanged, and the function can change in an exactly recomputable way; we verify that each evaluated permutation does. C2 separates fingerprinting from computation only under three conditions, which we state as an eligibility protocol: a strategy restricted to C2-invariant summaries must already predict the target; C2 must change a property specific to the rewiring; and the reader's identity must be attested. No natural setting we tested is eligible. On tiny MLPs a frontier reader route followed the rewired function on all 24 networks, but invariant baselines were near chance and its backend could not be attested.

### 15. Revisiting Continual Learning in Language Models: A Three-Interface Diagnosis of Stability and Plasticity

- **arXiv ID**: [2611.30151](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.30151v1)
- **Authors**: Keuntae Kim, Beomseok Lee, Hyunwoo Kim, Yong Suk Choi
- **Categories**: cs.LG, cs.AI, stat.ML
- **Published**: 2026-11-23
- **Relevance**: abstract:distillation
- **Abstract**: Continual learning in language models is commonly studied in the incremental learning (IL) setting, where a model learns a sequence of tasks and is evaluated through its final classifier or language-model (LM) head. This interface, however, does not indicate where forgetting occurs: a drop in accuracy may reflect representation degradation in the backbone, readout mismatch in the head, or both. We therefore argue that backward transfer (BWT) and forward transfer (FWT) should be interpreted as interface-dependent quantities rather than as single modellevel properties. We study three evaluation interfaces: the trained head, which is updated sequentially with the backbone; a probe newly trained on frozen backbone features; and a classifier-free backbone diagnostic. They measure deployed performance, recoverability under a new readout, and representation separability, respectively.
