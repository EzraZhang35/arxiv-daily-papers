- **Relevance**: title:tool use, title:tool creation, title:agent, abstract:tool use, abstract:agent, abstract:multi-turn
- **Abstract**: Searching text-rich knowledge graphs requires following relations while examining textual evidence. Predefined graph interfaces constrain what evidence agents can access, even when they write code to combine tool calls or process retrieved subgraphs. We propose direct knowledge graph interaction (DKGI), a harness in which language model agents create graph access tools directly over complete node and edge files. ### 3. Beyond Corrected Actions: Training LLM Agents on Textual Critiques

- **Categories**: cs.CL, cs.AI
- **Published**: 2026-10-14
- **Relevance**: title:agent, title:LLM agent, title:LLM, abstract:distillation, abstract:agent, abstract:multi-turn
- **Abstract**: Large language models (LLMs) are increasingly used for multi-turn agentic tasks that require interactions with users and environments over extended sequences of actions. A common approach to improving LLM agents is to use more capable expert models to provide training supervision, either by behavior cloning the expert's trajectories or by using the expert to correct the actions in trajectories generated by the weaker student model. However, expert LLMs can provide richer supervision than actions alone: they can directly generate textual critique that explain why specific actions are incorrect. We study whether training on such feedback benefits LLM agents and propose Critique-Augmented Agentic Fine-Tuning (CAFT), a framework that elicits textual expert feedback on student-generated trajectories. For each incorrect action, the expert yields both a corrected action and a textual critique explaining the student's mistake--which we then use as the training target for fine-tuning.

- **Published**: 2026-10-14
- **Relevance**: title:agent, title:LLM agent, title:LLM, abstract:function calling, abstract:agent
- **Abstract**: Post-training binds the competence of an LLM agent to the interface it was trained on. Semantics-preserving perturbations of that interface, such as renaming an action or changing the notation, leave the underlying task unchanged yet make the agent worse at it. Scoring each sample independently means interface randomization only broadens this dependence from a single interface to the training interface distribution, so out-of-distribution perturbations still degrade the agent. We propose Cross-Interface Policy Regularization (CIPR), a self-supervised objective that replaces action names with uninformative identifiers and presents each training sample under two interfaces in which equivalent actions are identified by construction. CIPR penalizes the KL divergence between the action distributions induced under the two interfaces, so the policy behaves consistently across them.

- **Categories**: cs.LG, cs.AI
- **Published**: 2026-10-13
- **Relevance**: title:distillation, abstract:tool use, abstract:on-policy distillation, abstract:distillation, abstract:agent, abstract:multi-turn
- **Abstract**: On-policy distillation (OPD) provides dense token-level supervision on student rollouts, but this supervision can become unreliable when the student and teacher diverge, either because of a large initial policy gap or compounding errors in long-horizon reasoning and tool use. We introduce Divergence-Triggered Dynamic Distillation (DTDD), an adaptive OPD framework that uses segment-level student-teacher divergence to trigger teacher takeover during training. When divergence triggers an intervention during reasoning, the student prefix continues to receive standard OPD supervision. The teacher then generates a recovery suffix, which the student learns through behavior cloning, using cross-entropy on the teacher-generated tokens. We evaluate DTDD in three regimes: long-horizon reasoning, distillation from a base initialization without offline supervised fine-tuning (SFT), and multi-turn agents.

### 7. Recovering Reliable Supervision via Critique-Guided On-Policy Distillation

- **Categories**: cs.LG, cs.AI
- **Published**: 2026-10-14
- **Relevance**: title:on-policy distillation, title:distillation, abstract:on-policy distillation, abstract:distillation, title:on-policy
- **Abstract**: On-policy distillation (OPD) trains a student model on its own rollouts with token-level supervision from a teacher, assuming that the teacher can reach a correct answer from any prefix the student writes. We call this property the recoverability of the teacher and show it fails on incorrect rollouts: the teacher follows student errors, although it recognizes them. We propose CriticOPD, using error recognition to restore recoverability. The teacher critiques an incorrect rollout, allowing the student to rewrite its solution following the error. The resulting output, including the critique as privileged information, is scored by the teacher while the student uses the revision without the critique for training.

### 8. RM-OPD: On-Policy Distillation for Generative Reward Models

- **Categories**: cs.LG, cs.CL
- **Published**: 2026-10-14
- **Relevance**: title:on-policy distillation, title:distillation, abstract:on-policy distillation, abstract:distillation, title:on-policy
- **Abstract**: Reinforcement Learning on Mixed-domain preference data (Mix-RL) has become the mainstream approach for training general-purpose Generative Reward Models (GRMs). However, Mix-RL suffers from two critical bottlenecks: the reward sparsity induced by outcome rewards, and the cross-domain interference arising from jointly optimizing heterogeneous capabilities, which together give rise to imbalanced capability acquisition and pervasive reward hacking. Inspired by the success of On-Policy Distillation (OPD) in multi-domain capability integration, we introduce RM-OPD (Reward Modeling via On-Policy Distillation), a two-stage framework that separates specialist acquisition from capability integration. Across multiple RM benchmarks and two base models, RM-OPD consistently outperforms vanilla Mix-RL and other strong baselines, achieving overall superior performance and exhibiting an emergent "teacher-surpassing" effect.

- **Relevance**: title:on-policy distillation, title:distillation, abstract:on-policy distillation, abstract:distillation, title:on-policy
- **Abstract**: On-policy distillation (OPD) transfers knowledge from a teacher model to a student along student-generated trajectories, typically by minimizing the reverse Kullback-Leibler (RKL) divergence. However, RKL can provide weak corrective signals for teacher-supported tokens to which the student assigns very low probability. This raises two questions: which positions are most consequential for the behavioral gap between the student and teacher, and what additional supervision is needed at these positions? Through cross-sampling, we find that divergence-based routing more effectively pinpoints positions contributing to the teacher-student performance gap than entropy-based routing. Further analysis reveals teacher-mode under-coverage at high Jensen-Shannon (JS) divergence positions, where standard OPD increases the large teacher-student probability gaps.

- **Abstract**: On-policy distillation (OPD) is increasingly studied as a cheaper alternative to reinforcement learning, yet it remains a form of knowledge distillation (KD) that relies on teacher supervision. - **Abstract**: Reinforcement learning (RL) has emerged as the primary paradigm for optimizing Large Language Model (LLM) agents, yet efficient critic-free algorithms (e.g., GRPO) suffer from a fundamental uniform credit assignment dilemma that broadcasts trajectory-level advantages equally to all actions. ### 18. Simplifying and Improving On-Policy Distillation for Few-Step Diffusion

- **arXiv ID**: [2610.18472](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18472v1)
- **Authors**: Jiawei Zhang, Ziyu Wan, Nanye Ma, Long Zhao, Dong Chen, Hongyu Liu, Qifeng Chen, Dongdong Chen

- **Published**: 2026-10-13
- **Relevance**: title:on-policy distillation, title:distillation, abstract:distillation, title:on-policy
- **Abstract**: Few-step distillation reduces the sampling cost of diffusion and flow models, but existing approaches often require auxiliary score models or specialized architectures. We show that simple on-policy velocity regression already provides a strong foundation for 4-NFE generation. To represent teacher velocity variation within each coarse transition, we introduce Pivot, a training-only residual proxy that separates local corrections from the student's interval-mean prediction.
