### 1. Proposing Better Rollouts for On-Policy Distillation

- **arXiv ID**: [2610.36477](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.36477v1)
- **Authors**: Sirui Chen, Yunzhe Qi, Haobo Xu, Jiaru Zou, Mengting Ai, Zihao Li, Jingrui He
- **Categories**: cs.LG, cs.AI
- **Published**: 2026-10-27
- **Relevance**: title:on-policy distillation, title:distillation, abstract:on-policy distillation, abstract:distillation, title:on-policy
- **Abstract**: On-policy distillation (OPD) improves language-model post-training by letting the student learn from its own sampled trajectories under teacher supervision, aligning the training process with inference-time behavior. However, the rollout policy in OPD is usually treated as fixed: each round samples directly from the current student. We show that this choice can create a compounding teacher--student mismatch, because it requires the teacher to provide effective supervision on whichever trajectories the student samples, including ones less reachable by the teacher itself. On such trajectories, teacher supervision can become noisy or even misleading, leading to undesirable student patterns such as repetition. Moreover, since the updated student in turn defines the rollout policy of subsequent rounds, these patterns persist and potentially amplify over training.

### 2. Concordat: Typed Sessions for LLM Agents

- **arXiv ID**: [2610.37461](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37461v1)
- **Authors**: Milad Sikaroudi
- **Categories**: cs.AI, cs.MA
- **Published**: 2026-10-27
- **Relevance**: title:agent, title:LLM agent, title:LLM, abstract:agent
- **Abstract**: Agents built on large language models are increasingly allowed to take consequential actions: cancelling bookings, issuing refunds, changing accounts. The rules that govern those actions, such as "obtain the customer's explicit confirmation before changing anything", are usually stated in the prompt and followed only as reliably as the model chooses to follow them. We present Concordat, a Python library that treats an agent as an object whose interface is a finite-state protocol, in the spirit of typestate and session types, and enforces that protocol on the model at run time. On every turn the model is offered only the tools that are legal in the current protocol state, calls outside that set are rejected before they have side effects, decisions reserved for humans can only be requested, and token and tool-call budgets are charged and split linearly across handoffs. Protocols are checked when they are defined, and a subclass of an agent is rejected if it could bypass its parent's protocol, a behavioural form of Liskov substitution.

- **Categories**: cs.LG
- **Published**: 2026-10-27
- **Relevance**: title:tool-use, title:agent, abstract:agent
- **Abstract**: We explore how to build performant large language model (LLM) tool-use agents that complete complex, multi-step tasks. Specifically, we study how data composition in synthetically generated tool-use environments affects the training of such agents. To do so, we propose the SynthAPI pipeline, which explicitly controls the diversity of generated environments along the axes of tools, tasks, and the databases on which the tools operate. - **Categories**: cs.RO, cs.AI
- **Published**: 2026-10-27
- **Relevance**: title:tool use, abstract:tool use
- **Abstract**: Failure diagnosers for robot manipulation are increasingly vision--language models (VLMs), trained and evaluated on single-arm failures produced by perturbing a successful demonstration. Bimanual tool use adds failures of other kinds: a tool that slips out of its socket while still held, two correct gestures made at different times, and attempts that fail for two reasons at once. On a simulated bimanual tool-use task whose failure causes are read from the recorded trace, we measure what each sensing channel lets a diagnoser tell. Within two attempts, a repair policy that escalates when the cause is not identifiable repairs as many failures as one told the true cause.

### 12. CASEL: Consequence-Aware Selective Execution for Reliable Long-Horizon Tool-Using Agents

- **arXiv ID**: [2610.36570](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.36570v1)
- **Authors**: Yanyan Zhang, Fei Shen, Ting Yan, Hao Zhang
- **Categories**: cs.AI, cs.CR, cs.SE
- **Published**: 2026-10-27
- **Relevance**: title:agent, abstract:agent
- **Abstract**: Tool-using language agents increasingly modify external systems, yet most runtime safeguards allocate scrutiny by generic uncertainty scores or coarse mutation heuristics. We introduce CASEL (Consequence-Aware Selective Execution Layer), a model-agnostic runtime allocation policy that combines calibrated failure probability, state-dependent consequence, reversibility, ambiguity, and evidence freshness to choose, for each proposed tool call, among direct execution, verification, sandboxing, clarification, approval, and abstention by minimizing a per-mode cost. We formalize direct execution as selective prediction and state and test the validity conditions of a finite-sample threshold and a trajectory risk ledger. In a controlled simulation over 20 seeds with 120,000 test actions each, in which every method has the same intervention menu and the same compute cost, CASEL reduces harmful-side-effect trajectories from 14.75 percent for an uncertainty-ranked gate to 7.02 percent and mean normalized harm from 0.184 to 0.123. A gate ranked by the product of failure probability and consequence reaches similar harm (5.82 percent, 0.120) but sends 45 percent more actions to human approval, and removing reversibility-aware routing raises the rate to 15.53 percent.

### 13. Watermark Basins: Black-Box Detection of Distilled LLMs

- **arXiv ID**: [2610.37266](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37266v1)
- **Authors**: Hongyan Chang, Hady Elsahar, Tom Sander, Pierre Fernandez, Saeed Mahloujifar, Sylvestre-Alvise Rebuffi, Tomáš Souček, Tuan Tran
  *(and 5 more)*
- **Categories**: cs.CR, cs.CL, cs.LG
- **Published**: 2026-10-27
- **Relevance**: title:LLM, abstract:distillation
- **Abstract**: Proprietary language models and their reasoning traces are vulnerable to API-based distillation, where an adversary trains a student model on the teacher model's outputs. To obtain evidence of distillation, model providers can watermark the teacher's outputs, aiming to leave a radioactive signal in the student's outputs. Existing black-box detectors search for such radioactivity using token-level statistics of the suspect model's outputs. ### 14. What Survives Distillation? A Mechanistic Interpretability Study of GPT-2 and DistilGPT-2

- **arXiv ID**: [2610.37157](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37157v1)
- **Authors**: Nabeelah Faumi, Deshan Sumanathilaka
- **Categories**: cs.CL
- **Published**: 2026-10-27
- **Relevance**: title:distillation, abstract:distillation
- **Abstract**: Knowledge distillation compresses large language models into compact students that preserve aggregate metrics, yet the mechanistic consequences of depth-reduction distillation remain largely uncharacterized. This work applies linear CKA, logit lens analysis, attention pattern decomposition, sparse autoencoder feature statistics, and activation patching to compare GPT-2 and its six-layer student DistilGPT-2 across two canonical circuits: the Indirect Object Identification (IOI) circuit and the Greater-Than (GT) circuit. The results reveal a compression-collapse dichotomy: the IOI circuit suffers complete mechanistic collapse (DistilGPT-2: 35.2% accuracy vs. 94.8%, zero name-mover preservation), while the GT circuit survives with 87.7% task performance recovery through active remapping of the teacher's layer-9 comparison head into student layer~4 (mean Pearson r = 0.945). These findings demonstrate that circuit compressibility governs mechanistic survival under distillation, and that aggregate metrics are insufficient diagnostics for circuit integrity.

- **Abstract**: We propose HiLo-RD (High-to-Low Budget Representation Distillation) to improve masked diffusion language models (MDLMs) under a limited generation budget. - **Categories**: cs.AI
- **Published**: 2026-10-27
- **Relevance**: title:agent, abstract:agent
- **Abstract**: Self-improving AI systems can revise the designs that govern their own behavior. Yet existing approaches center improvement on selected parent agents and their evolutionary experience, leaving the broader population largely underused. ### 18. ProCerDCL: Query-Efficient Black-Box Probabilistic Verification of Deep Learning Compiler Optimization

- **arXiv ID**: [2610.36499](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.36499v1)
- **Authors**: Zexin Li, Weibo Zhang, Linyi Li, Arvind Easwaran, Simin Chen
- **Categories**: cs.SE, cs.AI, cs.PL
- **Published**: 2026-10-27
- **Relevance**: abstract:tool use
- **Abstract**: We present \tool, a query-efficient framework for black-box probabilistic verification. Given a fixed, sampleable input distribution and a binary property oracle, \tool assesses the property's violation probability $p$ against a threshold $θ$. Under independent sampling and stateless oracle behavior, acceptance asserts $p\leθ$, rejection asserts $p>θ+η$ for a user-specified separation margin, and an inconclusive verdict indicates insufficient evidence for either conclusion. The probability of each type of erroneous verdict is bounded by its specified error budget. To support this three-valued contract efficiently, \tool uses a single sequential test, eliminating the interval family required by the baseline.
