### 1. SWE-OPD: On-Policy Distillation for Agentic Coding

- **arXiv ID**: [2611.21020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21020v1)
- **Authors**: Yuhang He, Yanyu Chen, Chuanzhe Guo, Jie Zhu, Zichen Tian, Hongyu Ge, Zixuan Ren, Huang He
  *(and 6 more)*
- **Categories**: cs.AI, cs.LG
- **Published**: 2026-11-16
- **Relevance**: title:on-policy distillation, title:distillation, title:agent, title:agentic, abstract:on-policy distillation, abstract:distillation
- **Abstract**: In this work, we study on-policy distillation (OPD) for transferring agentic coding capabilities from a large teacher model to a smaller student model. Prior work has focused primarily on reasoning and short-horizon agentic tasks. We present SWE-OPD, a minimal OPD study for software engineering, where the student interacts with executable repositories and the teacher supervises the prefixes visited by the student. Across three teacher--student pairs, two training sources, and three SWE benchmarks, we find that on-policy supervision transfers coding capability: supervision from Qwen3.6-27B increases the SWE-bench Pro resolve rate of Qwen3.5-9B from 35.9% to 39.4%. Refreshing trajectories is not strictly required either, as an offline variant with cached teacher scores still improves over the student baselines while cutting end-to-end GPU-hours by 59.4%--72.6% and halving peak GPU allocation, at benchmark-dependent accuracy.

### 3. Streaming On-Policy Distillation: Decoupling Three Clocks for Stage-Wise Supervision Consumption

- **arXiv ID**: [2611.22298](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22298v1)
- **Authors**: Yun Zhu Da Wang
- **Categories**: cs.AI
- **Published**: 2026-11-17
- **Relevance**: title:on-policy distillation, title:distillation, abstract:on-policy distillation, abstract:distillation, title:on-policy
- **Abstract**: In on-policy distillation (OPD), response completion, supervision delivery, and parameter updates need not share the same granularity. We develop streaming OPD by decoupling these three clocks: a response repeatedly delivers newly generated segments while generation continues, retaining the full causal prefix at each delivery and computing loss only on new positions. A consumption protocol then organizes supervision budgets, position selection, staleness handling, and update scheduling, allowing control over quantity, coverage, and version lag across training stages. In GSM8K experiments distilling Qwen3-4B into Qwen3-0.6B with VERL, supervised-budget matching reduces the early gap between best test accuracies over seven common candidates from 4.55 to 2.42 points. Synchronous continuation from the same checkpoint reaches 73.39% best test accuracy over seven common candidates at u=384,...,576, versus 74.30% synchronous and 71.95% budget-matched (differences -0.91 and -2.35 points); an unpaired extended record reaches 76.12%.

### 4. Conflict-Aware Multi-Teacher On-Policy Distillation in Aligned Action Spaces for Cross-Game Generalization

- **arXiv ID**: [2611.21094](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21094v1)
- **Authors**: Jing Yi, Cehao Yang, Hualei Zhang, Haitao Li
- **Categories**: cs.AI
- **Published**: 2026-11-16
- **Relevance**: title:on-policy distillation, title:distillation, abstract:on-policy distillation, abstract:distillation, title:on-policy
- **Abstract**: Zero-shot cross-game generalization aims to consolidate accessible source-game experts into a single vision-language model (VLM) that controls unseen games from raw pixels without target-game parameter updates. While multi-teacher on-policy distillation (MOPD) mitigates offline exposure bias and single-game reinforcement learning (RL) overfitting, scaling it to autoregressive VLMs faces two bottlenecks. First, token-to-action misalignment from subword length bias distorts discrete action probabilities and uncertainty margins. Second, multi-teacher policy conflict arises as source teachers under incompatible dynamics issue contradictory closed-loop corrections, causing negative transfer. We propose CAMOPD (Conflict-Aware Multi-Teacher On-Policy Distillation), a post-training framework that resolves multi-teacher policy conflict within an aligned semantic action space.

### 5. Rethinking Supervision Scaling in On-Policy Distillation: From Dense Supervision to Stochastic Horizons

- **arXiv ID**: [2611.20687](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.20687v1)
- **Authors**: Xinhao Yao, Zhenxing Dou, Yong Liu
- **Categories**: cs.LG
- **Published**: 2026-11-16
- **Relevance**: title:on-policy distillation, title:distillation, abstract:on-policy distillation, abstract:distillation, title:on-policy
- **Abstract**: On-policy distillation (OPD) provides dense token-level supervision along student-generated trajectories, yet the learning benefits of longer trajectories may not scale proportionally with their computational cost. We investigate how supervision utility evolves with trajectory length. Controlled experiments reveal diminishing returns from additional supervised tokens, while showing that later positions remain learnable and exhibit substantial cross-position supervision transfer. Gradient analysis further identifies shared update components across response positions and diverse position-specific residuals whose cross-rollout consistency decreases at greater depths. Motivated by these findings, we propose Stochastic Horizons On-Policy Distillation (SH-OPD), which reduces redundant supervision by randomly varying rollout horizons while preserving access to deeper response states.

- **Categories**: cs.AI
- **Published**: 2026-11-16
- **Relevance**: title:tool-use, title:agent, abstract:agent
- **Abstract**: Tool-use agents must chain interdependent calls, yet which outputs satisfy which later inputs in a deployed catalog is not something a general-purpose language model reliably infers from tool descriptions alone. We compile that structure once into a reusable external representation. ToolGraph is a directed, weighted dependency graph over a tool catalog: parameter matching and interventional probes, which execute a candidate chain with and without its proposed predecessor, propose edges whose evidence is combined into an edge confidence. Prior work uses such graphs to retrieve tools, search for tool paths, or score the next call. We ask instead whether the same structure can supply a \emph{multi-step} value by composing graph paths into a goal-conditioned action value \(Q(s,a,g)\).

- **Categories**: cs.CL, cs.AI
- **Published**: 2026-11-17
- **Relevance**: title:agent, abstract:agent
- **Abstract**: In repository-level question answering (QA), an agent explores a code repository with tools and should answer a question once enough information is gathered. Nevertheless, we find that LLM agents often continue to invoke tools even after the gathered information is already sufficient to answer. We call this phenomenon over-exploration. Moreover, even though existing methods improve task performance through supervised fine-tuning (SFT) on expert trajectories, SFT also propagates the over-exploration in the trajectories to the agent. ### 14. Not All Evidence Is Admissible: Evidence Governance for Tool-Augmented Medical Agents

- **arXiv ID**: [2611.22169](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22169v1)
- **Authors**: Runxun Zhang, Onofrio Antonio Catalano, Jinqiao Wang, Jingwei Wei
- **Categories**: cs.AI, cs.CV
- **Published**: 2026-11-17
- **Relevance**: title:agent, abstract:agent
- **Abstract**: Tool-augmented medical agents treat whatever their tools return as evidence. We show that this default is costly: medical tools fail silently, and a frozen reasoner cannot ignore what enters its context, so many cases it would have answered correctly are flipped to wrong once tool outputs are added. We call this evidence hijacking. $\mathcal{V}$-information explains the phenomenon: the reasoner has lost the option to ignore, so a tool output can carry negative usable information, and moving the decision of what to keep outside the reasoner restores that option. The theory further shows that part of any filtering gain comes merely from letting the reasoner see less, and only selection by content lets it see better.

- **Abstract**: Tool-using AI agents change files, databases, access rights and public information, yet permission to call a tool does not establish whether a mistake can be undone.
