### 1. Asymmetric On-Policy Distillation Learns In-Context Exploration

- **arXiv ID**: [2610.30105](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30105v1)
- **Authors**: Sriyash Poddar, Yanda Bao, Jacob Krantz, Matthew Chang, Xavier Puig, Roozbeh Mottaghi, Natasha Jaques, Abhishek Gupta
- **Categories**: cs.RO, cs.AI, cs.LG
- **Published**: 2026-10-21
- **Relevance**: title:on-policy distillation, title:distillation, abstract:distillation, abstract:agent, title:on-policy
- **Abstract**: Sequential decision-making under partial observability is difficult because agents must explore to infer hidden, task-relevant information before acting effectively. Learning such information-gathering behaviors tabula rasa via online reinforcement learning is sample-inefficient, whereas offline imitation averages expert actions across hidden states and fails to learn exploration. In this work, we propose ASTEROID, a simple and effective student--teacher supervised learning framework that can learn test-time exploration. Although a privileged expert never needs to explore, we demonstrate that a history-conditioned student can learn to do so when trained with on-policy contexts: the student first collects on-policy context under partial observability, and the privileged expert provides action labels conditioned on that context. By distilling expert actions directly conditioned on the student's on-policy history, the student learns to systematically resolve partial observability, i.e., search while uncertain and exploit once the hidden state is revealed.

- **Categories**: cs.AI, cs.CL, cs.LG
- **Published**: 2026-10-21
- **Relevance**: title:distillation, abstract:distillation, title:on-policy
- **Abstract**: On-policy self-distillation (OPSD) enables a language model to learn from a Teacher instantiated from the same model but given access to privileged information, such as a reference solution. We find that access to sufficient privileged information allows the Teacher to generate highly accurate trajectories; however, accuracy alone does not guarantee that these trajectories are suitable for Student learning. Direct access to a complete solution may encourage shortened, reference-conditioned derivations that omit the exploration, verification, and self-correction required to solve problems without privileged information. We introduce TeachBack, a bidirectional preference-learning framework for on-policy self-distillation that learns not only from the Teacher but also how the Teacher should teach. TeachBack shapes the Teacher's generation behavior by controlling the length of the reference-reasoning suffix injected into the prompt and by varying the prompt style.

### 10. Value-Rectified Distillation for Flow-based Offline Reinforcement Learning

- **arXiv ID**: [2610.29075](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.29075v1)
- **Authors**: Ke Jiang, Wen Jiang, Yoshinobu Kawahara, Xiaoyang Tan
- **Categories**: cs.LG, cs.AI
- **Published**: 2026-10-21
- **Relevance**: title:distillation, abstract:distillation
- **Abstract**: We aim to address the problem of Out-of-Distribution (OOD) Mode Averaging, a critical problem that emerges when distilling Flow Matching (FM) based behavior policies in offline Reinforcement Learning (RL): When learning over multi-modal datasets, standard distillation inadvertently forces the policy to interpolate across different behavioral modes, causing the generated actions to collapse into invalid, OOD regions. To resolve this, we propose Value-Rectified Distillation (VRD), a novel framework that reformulates offline policy distillation as a value-guided generative trajectory alignment problem. By employing a dynamic coupling mechanism, VRD actively rectifies flow dynamics to explicitly decouple distinct behavioral modes, cleanly isolating high-value actions. Specifically, we instantiate VRD through two complementary algorithms: VRD-Q, which utilizes value-quantile behavior distillation to statistically filter out low-value behaviors, and VRD-R, which reorganizes the latent space to geometrically isolate high-value generative trajectories from the FM-based behavior policy. Extensive empirical evaluations demonstrate that VRD improves existing generative baselines across diverse continuous control and planning tasks on the D4RL, OGBench, and Minari benchmarks, especially over those multi-modal, non-expert datasets.

### 16. Evolving with Open Worlds: Towards Recursive Self-Improvement for Future Forecasting

- **arXiv ID**: [2610.29784](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.29784v1)
- **Authors**: Zhipeng Chen, Yunsen Liang, Yanxi Chen, Hongkang Zhao, Lingyun Feng, Ruiyang Ren, Guoxin Chen, Jia Deng
  *(and 7 more)*
- **Categories**: cs.CL
- **Published**: 2026-10-21
- **Relevance**: abstract:agent
- **Abstract**: Most contemporary artificial intelligence (AI) systems are released with frozen parameters and improve through external updates, whereas an ideal system would keep improving from its own experience, a capacity we call recursive self-improvement (RSI). Yet studying RSI requires an environment that is continuously changing, objectively verifiable, and naturally grounded, posing a fundamental challenge for agent evolution. Here we introduce DeepEvolve, a sustainable evolution agent system grounded in future event prediction, in which agents continuously interact with a dynamically evolving environment and improve by internalizing and externalizing the experience gained through these interactions. On the external FutureX leaderboard, which evaluates predictions against real-world outcomes and is updated weekly, our self-improved forecaster ranked first across all systems over three consecutive weeks, achieving up to a 195% improvement in rating.

### 17. RCSD: Reinforcement and Continual Learning for Domain-Specific Speculative Decoding

- **arXiv ID**: [2610.29527](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.29527v1)
- **Authors**: Mingkang Liu, Weiming Meng, Peipei Jiao, Jiawei Zhang, Tianyuan Liu, Junyan Li, Lichen Pang
- **Categories**: cs.CL
- **Published**: 2026-10-21
- **Relevance**: abstract:distillation
- **Abstract**: Speculative decoding accelerates large language model inference by employing a compact draft model to propose candidate tokens that are verified in parallel by a target model. Conventional training relies on soft-label distillation, which forces the draft model to mimic the target's full output distribution, including high-entropy regions that degrade acceptance rates and increase early rejection risk. We propose RCSD (Reinforcement and Continual Learning for Speculative Decoding), a training framework that aligns draft model optimization with the system-level objective of maximizing acceptance length. RCSD leverages naturally occurring accepted and rejected sequences during speculative inference to construct implicit preference pairs, with masked loss to avoid penalizing target-aligned tokens. Evaluated across multiple benchmarks, RCSD improves average acceptance length by 35% over HASS and achieves 1.35x faster end-to-end inference than HASS on practical workloads, without compromising generation quality.

### 18. Target Regeneration Shrinks the Gains of Draft-Training Objectives in Speculative Decoding

- **arXiv ID**: [2610.29013](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.29013v1)
- **Authors**: Jungseob Lee, Hogun Seong, Euijun Choi, Sugyeong Eo
- **Categories**: cs.CL, cs.LG
- **Published**: 2026-10-21
- **Relevance**: abstract:distillation
- **Abstract**: Speculative decoding accelerates large language models by verifying drafted tokens in parallel. New draft-training objectives are usually credited with the acceptance they add over cross-entropy on externally written responses, yet many pipelines let the target rewrite them. We ask how much of that gain survives. With a vision-language target and a block drafter, we train three objectives on four versions of one corpus: cross-entropy on corpus tokens, token-level distillation from the target, and distillation gated to positions that would survive verification. Distillation and the gate add large gains on external responses, but after greedy regeneration, distillation adds a smaller gain and the gate little or none.
