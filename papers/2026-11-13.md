### 1. ToolTurns: Benchmarking LLM Agents in Multi-Turn Dialogue

- **arXiv ID**: [2611.14925](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14925v1)
- **Authors**: Milad Gholami, Brendan King, Jeffrey Flanigan
- **Categories**: cs.CL
- **Published**: 2026-11-11
- **Relevance**: title:agent, title:multi-turn, title:LLM agent, title:LLM, abstract:tool use, abstract:agent
- **Abstract**: Tool-using language models are increasingly deployed in conversational settings, yet most large-scale tool-use benchmarks focus on isolated requests. We introduce ToolTurns, a benchmark of 2,620 problems spanning 1,827 tools and 11,004 API endpoints across 47 categories. Each dialogue begins with an intentionally underspecified request and continues through two context-dependent follow-ups, testing clarification, tool use, and retention of conversational context. We also introduce an open-source user simulator that reveals withheld context through dialogue and generates follow-up requests, with a checker--fixer pipeline for improving its turns. Across nine language models, the best agent resolves 69.6% of individual requests but completes only 39.8% of full dialogues.

### 2. EvoX: Rethinking the Agent Harness as a Runtime Policy

- **arXiv ID**: [2611.15006](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.15006v1)
- **Authors**: Haoyang Zhang, Qumeng Sun, Xiao Zhang, Xiang Liu, Zhidong Fan, Junjie Wang
- **Categories**: cs.AI, cs.SE
- **Published**: 2026-11-11
- **Relevance**: title:agent, abstract:agent
- **Abstract**: Large language model (LLM) agents increasingly interact with tools and environments, yet plausible model decisions do not always translate into successful execution due to tool-call mismatches, interrupted workflows, and unsupported completion claims. We present EvoX, a Rust-based, multi-model agent runtime guided by The Second Policy, which views the harness as an Operational Policy complementing the foundation model's Semantic Policy. EvoX combines model-facing guidance with deterministic runtime control through three complementary mechanisms: Action Realization, Execution Continuity, and Outcome Grounding. To evaluate harnesses without suppressing their native behaviors, we introduce Contrast, a controlled and auditable framework integrating explicit fairness constraints, provider-bound request verification, and evidence-based adjudication. Across five benchmarks, six foundation models, and six agent harnesses, EvoX achieves the highest numerical success rate of 56.2% over 691 common task--model pairs, exceeding the average of the other five harnesses by approximately 3.0 percentage points.

### 3. When Safety Becomes an Attack Surface: Manipulating Abstention in Tool-Using AI Agents

- **arXiv ID**: [2611.14864](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14864v1)
- **Authors**: Charles Xiao
- **Categories**: cs.CR, cs.AI
- **Published**: 2026-11-11
- **Relevance**: title:agent, abstract:agent
- **Abstract**: An AI agent's decision not to act can itself become an attack surface. We study warning-induced over-abstention, an availability attack in which adversaries place fabricated risk warnings in low-trust content to prevent legitimate task completion. Our central premise is that safe abstention requires evaluating both the claimed risk and the authority of the evidence supporting it. We develop controlled counterfactual task variants that vary risk content, source provenance, and warning wording, and evaluate a Verify-Before-Abstain protocol that corroborates warnings against authoritative records or the underlying content. Across three model families and 51 injectable AgentAbstain task pairs, caution-first prompting amplifies the attack.

### 4. Effective Teacher Support for Reasoning Distillation

- **arXiv ID**: [2611.14850](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14850v1)
- **Authors**: Lavinia Ghita, Dhruv Desai
- **Categories**: cs.AI, cs.CL, cs.LG
- **Published**: 2026-11-11
- **Relevance**: title:distillation, abstract:distillation
- **Abstract**: Forward-KL language-model distillation normally computes dense teacher and student scores over the full vocabulary at every supervised position, making training and stored teacher targets expensive. We instead distill from the teacher's highest-probability vocabulary fraction, renormalizing the retained mass as we sweep support from 100% to 0.01%. Our main study distills Qwen3 4B -> 1.7B. We confirm the broader support-reduction finding on larger Qwen3 8B -> 4B and Nemotron Nano 12B -> 9B pairs and under answer-only supervision. We evaluate generation primarily on MMLU-Pro and secondarily on GPQA-Diamond.

### 5. Speech Tokenizers are Vulnerable: Transferable Semantic Attack and Robust Tokenizer

- **arXiv ID**: [2611.14929](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14929v1)
- **Authors**: Zhisheng Zhang, Yifan Mi, Yixuan Zhou, Haiyun Li, Shengbo Cai, Dongfu Song, Ziang Xu, Jie Hao
  *(and 1 more)*
- **Categories**: eess.AS, cs.AI, cs.CR, cs.SD
- **Published**: 2026-11-11
- **Relevance**: abstract:distillation
- **Abstract**: Speech tokenizers serve as speech front ends, encoding input speech signals into representations for downstream models, such as automatic speech recognition (ASR) systems and large audio-language models (LALMs). In this paper, we find that speech tokenizers are fragile at the semantic level: small perturbations can disrupt their semantic encoding process and consequently cause severe degradation in downstream ASR and LALM performance. To study this vulnerability, we propose T-SemAttack, a transferable semantic attack based on a semantic encoder ensemble. By jointly drifting the representation spaces of multiple semantic encoders, T-SemAttack effectively disrupts the semantic content of speech while preserving perceptual quality, thereby inducing error features. We further analyze layer-wise representation drift and cross-model gradient alignment.
