### 1. Capable Teacher, Myopic Scorer: Reward-Free Teacher Repair for Agentic On-Policy Distillation

- **arXiv ID**: [2610.28586](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28586v1)
- **Authors**: Shu Zhao, Tan Yu
- **Categories**: cs.CL, cs.AI
- **Published**: 2026-10-20
- **Relevance**: title:on-policy distillation, title:distillation, title:agent, title:agentic, abstract:on-policy distillation, abstract:distillation
- **Abstract**: Agentic on-policy distillation (OPD) trains a student agent on its own trajectories while a teacher scores every token. A natural explanation of where it goes wrong is compounding error: the student's mistakes carry its trajectories beyond the region where the teacher is reliable, so recent methods keep training inside that region, through episode-length curricula, teacher-guided turns, prefix replay, or teacher intervention where the two disagree. We show this explanation is incomplete. From the states where the student departs most from the teacher, the teacher's own takeovers still finish the task, yet the student trained on its token scores keeps running out of turns. We attribute this to the myopia of next-token scoring.

### 2. Ask to Distill: Learning to Synthesize Data for On-Policy Distillation

- **arXiv ID**: [2610.27450](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.27450v1)
- **Authors**: Jiashuo Sun, Hao Zhang, Xueqiang Xu, Heng Wang, Yixuan Xie, Pengcheng Jiang, Zhenrui Yue, Jiajun Fan
  *(and 4 more)*
- **Categories**: cs.AI, cs.CL
- **Published**: 2026-10-20
- **Relevance**: title:on-policy distillation, title:distillation, abstract:on-policy distillation, abstract:distillation, abstract:agent, title:on-policy
- **Abstract**: On-policy distillation (OPD) can succeed with surprisingly few training questions, motivating a view of questions as carriers of distributional knowledge transfer. How can we assess whether a newly generated question is valuable for distillation, and learn to generate better questions---without access to its correct answer? We introduce Ask-to-Distill (\method), a framework that treats synthetic questions as distribution elicitors and optimizes the teacher supervision they expose along student-generated trajectories. A student-initialized synthesizer is optimized using a distributional surrogate for question value, computed from a frozen student--teacher pair without reference answers, correctness rewards, or per-candidate student updates. The reward combines teacher--student disagreement with teacher probability mass on the student's candidate support, together with length and diversity controls.

### 3. Adaptive Pullback: Reweighting Supervision at Penalty Jumps in On-Policy Distillation

- **arXiv ID**: [2610.28194](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28194v1)
- **Authors**: Guangyu Liu, Siyuan Gan, Shilong Zhou, Linjian Meng, Boyan Wang, Jing Huo
- **Categories**: cs.LG, cs.AI
- **Published**: 2026-10-20
- **Relevance**: title:on-policy distillation, title:distillation, abstract:on-policy distillation, abstract:distillation, title:on-policy
- **Abstract**: On-policy distillation (OPD) supervises student-generated responses using sampled-token student-teacher log-ratios. A positive log-ratio penalizes a token that the student assigns higher probability to than the teacher, but its current value does not distinguish sustained high penalties from abrupt increases along a response. We examine these local changes by comparing consecutive blocks of tokens within the same response. Our diagnostic identifies penalty jumps, abrupt increases in average penalty that persist on held-out tokens. Across these jumps, penalties rise while teacher confidence decreases.

### 4. Can Native Safety Travel? Reusing Safety Pretraining Across Models via On-Policy Distillation

- **arXiv ID**: [2610.27555](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.27555v1)
- **Authors**: Bingqi Shang, Yang Zhang, Yiwei Chen, Changsheng Wang, Shiyu Chang, Bingquan Shen, Sijia Liu
- **Categories**: cs.LG, cs.AI, cs.CL
- **Published**: 2026-10-20
- **Relevance**: title:on-policy distillation, title:distillation, abstract:on-policy distillation, abstract:distillation, title:on-policy
- **Abstract**: Safety pretraining has emerged as a promising paradigm for embedding safety deeply into a model during pretraining, yielding robustness to subsequent fine-tuning that conventional safety post-training often lacks. We refer to this safety acquired during model pretraining as native safety. Yet native safety is expensive to acquire, confined to the model in which it was pretrained, and often accompanied by lower general utility. In this work, we rethink the practical value of safety-pretrained models and introduce, for the first time, the native safety reuse problem: Can native safety be transferred from a specialized, potentially smaller, safety-pretrained "teacher" to standard-pretrained "students," enabling them to inherit its robustness while preserving their general utility? We show that native safety can "travel" through on-policy distillation.

### 5. SAPD: Learning from Agent Experience without Environment Interaction

- **arXiv ID**: [2610.27847](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.27847v1)
- **Authors**: Juncheng Diao, Haibin Wang, Wei Shi, Yifan Ding, Qingbin Li, Rongxiang Weng, Xunliang Cai
- **Categories**: cs.AI, cs.LG
- **Published**: 2026-10-20
- **Relevance**: title:agent, abstract:on-policy distillation, abstract:distillation, abstract:agent, abstract:multi-turn
- **Abstract**: Scaling the training of Large Language Model (LLM) agents typically requires a large collection of executable environments, whose construction, maintenance, and operation constrain the expansion of training experience. Existing offline trajectories offer an opportunity to reuse interaction experience, yet effectively refining the current policy without revisiting these environments remains challenging: supervised fine-tuning (SFT) can lead to catastrophic forgetting, while online reinforcement learning requires real-time interaction with the environment. To address this challenge, we propose Step-Advantage Policy Distillation (SAPD), a method for learning from offline trajectories using relative step advantages. SAPD decomposes multi-turn trajectories into individual decision contexts for on-policy distillation, allowing the student to generate responses conditioned on recorded histories, and introduces relative step advantage signals to stabilize training. The entire process requires neither new environment observations nor additional multi-turn interactions.

### 6. On-Policy Transition Distillation

- **arXiv ID**: [2610.28048](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28048v1)
- **Authors**: Runyang You, Yongqi Li, Tiezheng Yu, Wenjie Li
- **Categories**: cs.CL, cs.AI, cs.LG
- **Published**: 2026-10-20
- **Relevance**: title:distillation, abstract:on-policy distillation, abstract:distillation, title:on-policy
- **Abstract**: On-policy distillation (OPD) consolidates expert capabilities into a single student by matching teacher and student final-layer distributions on student-generated rollouts. We investigate whether depthwise prediction transitions, the changes in predictions between layers, can provide additional task-relevant supervision. Our diagnostic study finds that stronger teacher--student agreement in intermediate transitions is associated with higher answer correctness within questions, with correct responses exhibiting lower mean transition KL across all measured intermediate layer pairs. These observations motivate On-Policy Transition Distillation (OPTD), which augments final-layer distribution matching by aligning teacher and student layer-to-layer log-probability increments on student-generated rollouts. Evaluations across three backbones and eight benchmarks spanning mathematics, coding, and instruction following show that OPTD achieves the best or tied-best overall performance in both dual- and three-domain distillation.

### 7. Projected Distillation: On Learning to Solve Hard Problems from Close, Correct Targets

- **arXiv ID**: [2610.27467](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.27467v1)
- **Authors**: Shripad V. Deshmukh, Christian Bock, Kangrui Ruan, Abhijeet Awasthi, Vijay Lingam, Rein Houthooft, Manish Prajapat
- **Categories**: cs.LG, cs.AI
- **Published**: 2026-10-20
- **Relevance**: title:distillation, abstract:tool use, abstract:on-policy distillation, abstract:distillation
- **Abstract**: On-policy learning is central to post-training: training on a model's own generations limits distribution shift and helps preserve existing capabilities. On hard problems, however, on-policy RL provides no reward-based learning signal when all attempts fail. On-policy distillation provides next-token supervision from a teacher, but on hard problems, if a rollout goes wrong early, subsequent supervision is conditioned on prefixes that already contain the mistake. Correct behavior can instead come from a gold or teacher solution, but such off-policy targets can differ substantially from the model's generations and, as we show, may erode existing capabilities. We therefore treat on-policyness as a continuum and propose projected distillation, which minimally edits the student's failed attempts into correct targets.

### 8. Building Telecom Language Models with RSOD: Domain Data, Specialist Post-Training, Memory-Conscious Distillation, and Networking Evaluation

- **arXiv ID**: [2610.27993](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.27993v1)
- **Authors**: Anil Srirangapatna Nagesh, Nitin Gupta, Anuj Taneja, Vivek Singh, Shane Eleniak, Theo Zhao, Moulidharan N, Shashank Keshetty
- **Categories**: cs.AI, cs.CL, cs.LG
- **Published**: 2026-10-20
- **Relevance**: title:distillation, abstract:on-policy distillation, abstract:distillation
- **Abstract**: Telecom language models must combine broad networking knowledge with specialist behavior across routing, broadband services, access technologies, and operational diagnostics while remaining practical to deploy as a single model. We present an end-to-end framework that builds source-grounded synthetic training data from structured networking taxonomies and expert graphs, materializes 212,559 routed training records across fourteen networking routes, trains six frozen specialist LoRA teachers over one shared continued-pretrained backbone, and consolidates their behavior into one deployable student adapter using Routed Specialist On-Policy Distillation (RSOD). In RSOD, the committed student generates each trajectory, the route-assigned specialist teacher-forces the exact native token sequence, compact teacher hidden states are reconstructed through the shared frozen output head, and only the student LoRA is updated. A controlled reconstruction validation shows mean absolute logit error of 7.26 x 10^-6 with 100% top-1 agreement between native and reconstructed teacher logits. On four RTX 6000 Ada GPUs, the completed 2,000-step run produced 16,000 trajectories with zero observed student-version or token- ID mismatches.

### 9. Every Model Leaves a Trace: Multi-Resolution LLM Fingerprinting

- **arXiv ID**: [2610.28434](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28434v1)
- **Authors**: Mohammad Reza Modarres, Armin Tourajmehr, Artin Tavasoli, Yadollah Yaghoobzadeh, Mohammad Taher Pilehvar
- **Categories**: cs.CL, cs.AI
- **Published**: 2026-10-20
- **Relevance**: title:LLM
- **Abstract**: Large language models leave model-specific traces in their generated text, but it remains unclear how finely these fingerprints can distinguish related models and which signals remain informative as obvious cues are reduced. We study this through fingerprint resolution, considering both model resolution, from different families to distilled and adapted variants, and signal resolution across behavioral, stylometric, lexical, and semantic evidence. We progressively suppress surface cues, paraphrase outputs, vary the amount of available text, and test generalization across prompt distributions and languages. Our results show that fingerprints persist beyond broad family differences, remaining detectable in distilled models and even LoRA adaptations of the same base model. Although attribution weakens as surface and lexical cues are altered, substantial model-specific signal remains.

### 10. PAR: Progress-Aligned Recurrence for Length Generalization in State Tracking

- **arXiv ID**: [2610.27628](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.27628v1)
- **Authors**: Pan Wang, Yihao Hu, Guangyuan Dong, Yitong Lin, Hang Wang, Zimi Liu, Zhihao Wen, Jianshe Li
  *(and 3 more)*
- **Categories**: cs.CL
- **Published**: 2026-10-20
- **Relevance**: abstract:distillation
- **Abstract**: Looped language models can run more iterations at test time, but additional iterations need not help them solve longer problems. For sequential state tracking, final-answer supervision does not specify how far each iteration should advance, and repeated updates can change the scale of the recurrent state. We introduce \textbf{Progress-Aligned Recurrence (PAR)}, which trains the prediction at iteration $k$ to match the state after the first $k$ operations and rescales recurrent states to their first-iteration norms. On swap-$S_5$, a Qwen2.5-0.5B model trained on at most 32 moves achieves 95.5\% mean full-state accuracy at 512 moves, or $16\times$ the training length, compared with 55.7\% reported for RLT and 0.8\% for explicit chain of thought on the same backbone. Controlled $S_5$ ablations show that removing norm anchoring reduces the extrapolation factor from $16\times$ to below $2.2\times$, and replacing progress-aligned targets with final-answer supervision reduces it to at most $8\times$.

### 11. Large Flow Language Models

- **arXiv ID**: [2610.28559](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28559v1)
- **Authors**: Arnav Chavan, Chanhyuk Lee, Udbhav Bamba, Jaehoon Yoo, Seunghoon Hong, Aditi Raghunathan, Jinwoo Kim
- **Categories**: cs.CL, cs.AI
- **Published**: 2026-10-20
- **Abstract**: Continuous flow language models offer a path to parallel generation with tools for control and acceleration unavailable to discrete diffusion, yet effective techniques for building them at the billion-parameter scale remain less established. We present Lafa (Large flow language model), a fast, high-performing flow language model scaled to 8B parameters in a data-efficient manner. Lafa is a blockwise flow model with causal attention on the prefix and bidirectional, asynchronous flow within each block. This design enables efficient training by adapting pretrained autoregressive (AR) models and fast inference via prefix caching and blockwise parallel decoding. We train Lafa at 0.5B, 7B, and 8B scales with fewer than 30B tokens, more efficient than previous diffusion models, and evaluate it on instruction following, reasoning, and coding, where the learned flow matches or surpasses the quality of AR and can be distilled into flow maps for one- and few-step generation.

### 12. Training-Free Continual Learning for Multimodal Forecasting via Prompt Evolution

- **arXiv ID**: [2610.27468](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.27468v1)
- **Authors**: Laurent Mombaerts, Jonathan Taws, Jacopo Gargano, Dinesh Gaddam, Kevin George, Srijan Tiwari, Shreyas Rajesh
- **Categories**: cs.LG, cs.AI
- **Published**: 2026-10-20
- **Abstract**: Adapting a forecasting system to a changing world classically means retraining. We ask whether the task of continually learning regimes for time series forecasting can be represented by a piece of text: a guidance block, evolved by outcome-driven reflection and used in a frozen LLM forecaster's prompt. On a refreshed multimodal benchmark (weekly search indices, daily currencies, daily commodities, each window paired with web-retrieved news), we run GEPA-style prompt evolution under strict contamination control to prevent the data leakage common in timeseries benchmarks: the forecaster's knowledge cutoff predates every evaluated window. A 900-word evolved guidance carried by Llama-3.3-70B beats TimeSeries Foundation Models (TSFMs) on held-out data (GM MASE 0.776 vs. 0.898 for TabPFN-TS-3), transfers to more recent models without measurable loss, and costs dollars rather than GPU-days to update.
